# Throne and Liberty

A free-to-play open-world MMORPG (2023, NCSoft / Amazon Games) that replaces traditional mounts with instant player morphs into creatures, ties dynamic weather to tactical combat, and builds its endgame around massive guild castle sieges with hundreds of simultaneous participants.

## Design

- **Morph system instead of mounts.** Players transform instantly into animals to fly, swim, or charge across terrain — no mount summoning animation, no dismount delay. The golem morph is siege-specific: it can physically crash through castle walls. Morphs are functional traversal verbs, not cosmetic variations, and each form changes the skill bar entirely. This makes the transform system a tactical layer, not just travel QoL.
- **Dual-weapon classless progression.** Characters are defined by their two equipped weapons (sword+shield, longbow, crossbow, dagger, staff, wand, greatsword, etc.), not a class selection. Each weapon has its own skill tree; swapping weapons mid-combat switches skill bars. This allows the same character to spec differently for PvE dungeon runs versus siege PvP without rerolling, and enables a wide variety of hybrid builds without class-lock friction.
- **Dynamic weather as combat modifier.** Weather (rain, wind, fog, clear) changes gameplay substantively: rain floods sewers and cuts off access routes; wind affects arrow trajectories and can carry players to otherwise unreachable areas; some equipment and skills only activate in specific weather windows. The day/night cycle (4 hours day, 1 hour night in real time) spawns night-exclusive monsters and activates night-only item effects. Weather is not cosmetic atmosphere — it is a scheduled tactical variable.
- **Massive guild castle sieges.** Castle sieges support hundreds of players simultaneously in persistent open-world zones. The golem morph is specifically designed to enable dynamic assault: players can breach walls rather than waiting at a chokepoint. Guild ownership grants territory bonuses (tax income, resource access). Siege content is the primary endgame social driver.
- **Party dungeon content for casual co-op.** Alongside the large-scale PvP, the game added instance dungeons for small parties (5-player), built around mechanic-literacy: knowing and countering dungeon-specific patterns is the skill expression, not gear score alone. This created a tiered co-op offering: casual party content and prestige siege content, without requiring all players to engage with both.
- **Korean → Global adaptation.** Amazon Games handled the global version, removing features that conflicted with Western expectations (specifically, autoplay/auto-hunt was removed for the global launch). The adaptation process itself is a case study: what Korean F2P MMOs include as standard QoL (auto-routing, auto-combat) reads as pay-to-win to Western audiences even when it is not directly monetized.

## Implementation

- Developer: FirstSpark Games / NCSoft (Seoul, Korea); Publisher: NCSoft (Korea, December 7, 2023), Amazon Games (global, October 1, 2024).
- Engine: Unreal Engine 4. Large-scale simultaneous player counts in open-world siege zones required significant server infrastructure engineering; launch population (3 million players, peak #4 on Steam, 300,000 concurrent) strained server capacity visibly before consolidation.
- Free-to-play with cosmetic monetization; live-service updates. Server count consolidated from 107 to 25 by February 2025 as the initial spike normalized — a common pattern for Korean MMO global launches.

## Why it matters

Throne and Liberty demonstrates that the "no mounts" design space has a creative answer: if traversal is a morph into a creature with its own abilities and the transform is instant, travel becomes a tactical layer rather than a logistics chore. The dual-weapon classless system shows how to give players build identity without class-lock friction — the character is defined by what they equip, not what they chose at creation. The dynamic weather system is a rare example of environment as scheduled content: players plan around it, rather than treating it as background.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Combat]] — environment as tactical variable.** Wayfinder's dungeon design can borrow from T&L's weather model: chart affixes that activate environmental modifiers (flooded corridors, night-exclusive enemy spawns, wind-affected projectiles) would make the same dungeon layout feel different run-to-run without procedural generation overhead.
- **[[Trades and Leveling]] — weapon-based identity over class-lock.** If Wayfinder's Trades are less about class gating and more about which tools you bring into a run (analogous to which two weapons T&L characters equip), players can experiment with trade combinations per chart without rerolling — matching the cozy, low-friction exploration intent.
- **[[Multiplayer Co-op]] — tiered group content scope.** T&L's bifurcation (small party dungeons vs. large-scale sieges) maps to Wayfinder's potential: co-op chart runs for 2–4 players as the default, with community-scale summit events as prestige endgame — each satisfying without requiring the other.
