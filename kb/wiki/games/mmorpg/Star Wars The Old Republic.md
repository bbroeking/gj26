# Star Wars The Old Republic

A narrative-first MMORPG (2011, BioWare Austin / EA) that gambled a reported $150–200 million on making every character in an MMO fully voice-acted — and built the most expensive single-player game ever wrapped in MMO infrastructure, proving both the ceiling and the cost of story-as-a-service.

## Design

- **Fully voiced quests — the hook.** Every character, including player characters, features full voice dialogue. Eight distinct class storylines (four Republic, four Imperial) each deliver a three-act narrative that progresses with leveling. The design holds a Guinness World Record: largest entertainment voice-over project ever, with over 200,000 lines of recorded dialogue. This was the first MMO to treat its main quest as a cinematic RPG experience rather than text-and-go.
- **Faction story with moral autonomy.** Republic and Imperial factions each have four classes; a Light/Dark alignment system operates independently of faction, so an Imperial Agent can be light-side and a Jedi can fall to darkness. Player choices accumulate alignment points and alter companion relationships.
- **Companion system.** Each class acquires five companions through the class storyline. Companions are fully characterized with voiced dialogue, approval/disapproval meters tied to player choices, and romance options. They serve as combat partners (tank/heal/DPS role fills) and asynchronous crafting dispatchers — companions sent on missions return resources while the player adventures, a form of offline progression. When a group member disconnects mid-instance, their companion can substitute in, though with explicitly lower effectiveness for challenging content. BioWare added granular AI toggles per companion ability (auto/manual) in response to player feedback.
- **Subscription collapse.** SWTOR launched December 20, 2011, peaked at 1.7 million subscribers, then fell below 1 million within months. It transitioned to free-to-play in November 2012 — among the fastest major MMO subscription collapses at the time.

## Implementation

- Engine: HeroEngine (licensed from Simutronics), chosen for rapid online world development. Performance issues at launch (particularly in busy areas) were widely attributed to HeroEngine limitations.
- Voice production: 200+ voice actors, over 1,000 recording sessions, 200,000+ lines — representing a reported multi-million-dollar voice budget on top of an estimated $150–200M total development spend. By 2019 the game had generated ~$1B in lifetime revenue.
- BioWare Austin led development with BioWare Edmonton supporting. The studio was largely built for this project.
- The project was called "a massively insane expenditure and hugely complicated" by BioWare's own leaders in retrospect. It ran concurrently with Mass Effect 3 development.
- Transitioned to Broadsword Online Games in 2023.

## Why it matters

SWTOR is the clearest case study in the cost/benefit of narrative investment in an MMO: the voiced quest system created an unmatched first-playthrough experience but became prohibitively expensive to extend — each content update required another recording cycle. It also demonstrated that players churn after consuming the story even when mechanics are solid, because the MMO's repetitive systems aren't built to sustain the same engagement as the narrative layer. The companion-as-offline-worker model is an underrated design that survived SWTOR's subscription collapse and still serves the game.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Trades and Leveling]] and companion asynchronous work.** SWTOR companions dispatched on crafting missions while the player adventures is a model for Wayfinder's offline skilling — characters advancing Trades between sessions without requiring active play.
- **[[Economy]] and the cost of content.** SWTOR's voice-acting lock-in shows the danger of production costs that scale linearly with content; Wayfinder's procedural [[Chart Loop]] and parameterized [[Dungeon Generation]] are the right answer to sustainable content.
- **Story investment vs. replayability tension.** SWTOR's story was compelling; its repeated play loop was not. Wayfinder's [[Chart Loop]] must prioritize systemic replayability over authored narrative set-pieces — the opposite design emphasis.
