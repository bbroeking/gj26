# MapleStory

2D side-scrolling free-to-play MMORPG released April 2003 (Korea) / May 2005 (North America) by Wizet and published by Nexon, notable for pioneering the cash-shop monetisation model, a branching job-advancement class tree across 40+ distinct characters, and platforming as first-class dungeon content.

## Design

- **Job advancement:** Every character starts as a "Beginner" with no class. At level 10 players pick one of five base paths (Warrior, Mage, Bowman, Thief, Pirate). Further advancements at levels 30, 60, 100, 200, and 260 branch into specialisations. The branching tree across 40+ classes (Explorers, Cygnus Knights, Heroes, Resistance, Nova, Flora, and more) means the game's long-term playability comes from rerolling and exploring alternate paths, not deepening a single character.
- **2D platforming as content:** Jump Quests are challenge maps where players navigate moving platforms, avoid lasers and saw blades, and reach an NPC at the far end. This turns the movement system into a puzzle layer — unique among MMOs of its era and still distinctive today. Party Quests were early instanced co-op content long before instanced dungeons became standard.
- **XP grind:** Combat is straightforward ("spam two or three skills") against respawning monster fields. The loop is unambiguously a Skinner-box dopamine feed — level number going up is the core reward. Boss encounters involve minimal mechanical interaction; the challenge is gear and level gating.
- **Cash Shop (2004 precedent):** MapleStory is widely credited with popularising the F2P + cash-shop model in Western markets when Nexon brought it west in 2005. The shop sells cosmetic items (most originally time-limited, expiring after 30–90 days), plus enhancement helpers. The StarForce enhancement system adds RNG gear amplification with destruction risk — creating demand for reroll and protection cash-shop items.
- **Reboot mode:** A parallel server where trading between players is disabled but monster drop rates multiply 5x. Progression is self-contained — earned via Mesos rather than market purchases. Introduced to address the main server's pay-to-progress perception, but StarForce RNG still applies.
- **Character quantity over depth:** With 40+ classes, the game incentivises roster breadth. Rapid leveling was introduced so players can "reach their character's maximum potential more easily" — a convenience-versus-authenticity trade that reduces early-game exploration immersion.

## Implementation

- **Platform:** Windows and macOS, keyboard + mouse (heavy left-hand hotbar use). The 2D engine is Nexon's in-house Windows client, iteratively updated since 2003.
- **180 million registered users** by 2020; **$3 billion lifetime revenue** — making it one of the highest-grossing games of all time despite (or because of) its free-to-play structure.
- **Longevity through class releases:** Major content updates are primarily new class branches, each with their own story arc. This turns the character roster into an ongoing live-service content delivery mechanism.
- **Regional publishing model:** Nexon operates separate servers per region (KMS, GMS, MSEA, etc.) with some divergence in update cadence and cash-shop contents — an early demonstration of the costs of regional fragmentation.

## Why it matters

MapleStory proved that **2D side-scrolling is a viable MMO form factor** (not a concession) and that **F2P + cosmetic cash shop** can sustain a game for 20+ years. Its job-advancement tree is an enduring template for class-unlock progression that gives new players a goal (pick a path, reach the specialisation) without gating content. The platforming integration into dungeon quests is an underused idea: navigation difficulty as a content layer rather than just aesthetic.

The design failure lesson is equally valuable: combat that is mechanically thin ("spam same skills") makes the XP grind the only reward loop, which works via Skinner-box psychology but does not build genuine engagement. When the cash shop can meaningfully accelerate gear, the line between cosmetic and P2W collapses.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Job advancement → [[Trades and Leveling]]:** The Beginner → pick a path → specialise structure maps to Wayfinder's Trade design. Wayfinder's four Trades already mirror this: players start neutral and lean into Wayfinding, Earthcraft, or Wildcraft. The lesson is to keep the early-game path legible without forcing commitment too early.
- **Platforming as puzzle:** If Wayfinder ever includes [[Dungeon Generation]] layout that rewards movement skill (jump puzzles, timed traversal), MapleStory proves this is an MMO-compatible design — not a genre mismatch.
- **Cash-shop expiry:** MapleStory's time-limited cosmetics model drove re-purchase behaviour but frustrated players who lost items. Any [[Economy]] or cosmetic system in Wayfinder should decide deliberately whether cosmetics are permanent or consumable.
