# Harvest Moon

Farming simulation (1996, Marvelous/Victor Interactive / Natsume NA) — the genre-founding SNES title designed by Yasuhiro Wada that introduced the seasonal crop calendar, stamina-limited daily loop, livestock management, marriage system, and festival calendar that every subsequent farming sim inherits.

## Design

- **Seasonal calendar:** Four seasons, each approximately 30 in-game days. Crops are season-locked — spring vegetables cannot grow in summer. Fall has no plantable seeds in the original; winter forbids all outdoor farming. The season transition is the player's primary deadline: harvest before the clock turns or lose the crop.
- **Stamina system:** A visible stamina bar drains with every farm action — tilling, watering, harvesting, chopping wood, even walking. Reaching zero causes the player to collapse and wake up the next morning having lost time. Eating cooked food restores stamina. This single mechanic creates all meaningful daily decisions.
- **Livestock management:** Cows and chickens housed in barn and coop respectively. Animals require daily feeding (fodder from harvested grass) and affection (brushing, talking). Neglected animals produce lower-quality goods or stop producing entirely. Milk and eggs are the first repeatable income source.
- **Marriage and relationships:** Several bachelorettes in town can be courted by visiting them, giving gifts, and attending festivals. Marriage advances the family narrative — the eventual child serves as a soft "future" goal. Relationship-building is not required for farm success but rewards attentive social play.
- **Festival calendar:** Town-wide events (seasonal festivals, competitions) punctuate each season, providing social glue and minor gameplay stakes (crop competitions reward money and goodwill).
- **Time limit and endings:** The game runs for 2.5 in-game years (the original Japanese version ends with a scored evaluation based on farm prosperity, relationships, and livestock quality), producing multiple ending tiers. This finite structure distinguishes it from later infinite-sandbox descendants like Stardew Valley.
- **Cozy hook:** The "boring activity made fun" revelation — tending crops, talking to villagers, and attending festivals are mundane activities rendered satisfying by incremental feedback (crops grow visibly, villager affection displays progress). No combat, no fail state beyond missing a harvest.

## Implementation

- **Developer/publisher:** Developed by Victor Interactive Software (later rebranded Marvelous), designed by Yasuhiro Wada. Published in Japan by Marvelous (August 9, 1996 SNES), in North America by Natsume (June 1997 SNES).
- **Engine/tech:** SNES cartridge; no documented custom engine. The tile-based farm grid, day/night cycle, and seasonal palette swaps were novel applications of SNES hardware at the time.
- **Series trajectory:** The game spawned a franchise that split in 2014 into two parallel lines: *Story of Seasons* (Marvelous, the "true" Harvest Moon lineage) and *Harvest Moon* (Natsume, using the Western brand name for new games with different development). Over 30 games across both lines exist as of 2026.
- **Save model:** Single SNES save slot with battery backup.

## Why it matters

Harvest Moon is the ur-text of every life-sim and cozy game. It proved that **simulating domestic agricultural labor** could be engaging entertainment — a counterintuitive proposition in 1996. The three pillars it invented — **seasonal deadline pressure, stamina-gated daily loops, and relationship-as-progression** — appear in every descendant game. Stardew Valley, Rune Factory, My Time at Portia, and Wayfinder's own gathering system all trace a direct design lineage to this title. The finite-year structure also established the "evaluate your farm" end-loop that cozy games later softened into open-ended sandboxes.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Stamina as the core verb:** Harvest Moon's stamina bar is the ancestral form of Wayfinder's gather channel and energy-gated skilling. The design insight — "limited actions per day make every choice meaningful" — is load-bearing for [[Gathering]] and [[Trades and Leveling]]; the question is whether Wayfinder needs a global stamina pool or can rely on per-node channel times.
- **Seasonal scarcity as motivation:** Crops only grow in certain seasons; Wayfinder's Affix system could similarly make certain GatherNode types only appear in specific Chart configurations, creating analogous seasonal-scarcity motivation without a real calendar.
- **Festival calendar as social glue:** Even minimal NPC events (seasonal competitions, town gatherings) deepen investment in a place; Wayfinder's town of Bramblewood benefits from even light festival/event beats — see [[NPCs]] and [[Voice and Tone]].
