# Animal Crossing New Horizons

Life simulation (2020, Nintendo EPD / Nintendo) — a real-time island-building game where players develop a deserted island at their own pace, driven by DIY crafting, the Bells/Nook Miles dual economy, and the KK Slider concert as a soft narrative climax, selling over 47 million copies worldwide.

## Design

- **Real-time clock:** The game runs on the device's system clock, mirroring real-world time of day and calendar. Shops open and close, villagers follow schedules, seasonal events fire on real dates, and bugs/fish spawn by time and month. The world exists without the player — missing a day feels tangible, reinforcing habitual return.
- **DIY crafting:** Players gather natural resources (wood, stone, iron nuggets, clay, weeds, fruit) by hitting rocks and trees, then convert them at a workbench into tools, furniture, and décor. DIY recipes are discovered via balloon presents, villager gifts, and seasonal events. Tools are breakable, creating a steady resource pull even for veteran players.
- **Island rating and KK Slider unlock:** Isabelle rates the island on a 1–5 star scale based on furniture placement, flower density, and villager count. Reaching 3 stars triggers the KK Slider concert — the game's narrative "completion" beat. Post-concert, the Island Designer app unlocks terraforming (reshape cliffs and rivers), shifting from progression to pure creative expression.
- **Dual currency economy:** Bells (general currency — buy furniture, pay off home loans to Tom Nook) and Nook Miles (earned by completing daily/lifetime tasks, spent on premium travel tickets and rare items). Nook Miles incentivize trying every activity; Bells reward farming specific high-value crops (turnips via the stalk market).
- **Seasonal events:** Real-date holiday events (Bunny Day, Halloween, Toy Day) distribute limited furniture items and gate them behind real time, preventing time-travel exploitation and creating a FOMO calendar that drives daily login.
- **Villager relationships:** 400+ NPC villagers with distinct personalities and dialogue trees. Players invite villagers to their island via Nook Miles Tickets; departure is semi-random and emotionally charged. No formal gift-preference system — relationships are surface-level compared to Stardew Valley.
- **Multiplayer:** 4 players locally, 8 online per island. Friends can visit, trade, and give items. One island per Switch system was a controversial design choice — households share rather than each player having their own.

## Implementation

- **Developer:** Nintendo EPD (Entertainment Planning & Development), directed by Aya Kyogoku. Development began around 2012 after New Leaf shipped.
- **Platform:** Nintendo Switch exclusive at launch (Switch 2 version with 4K and mouse controls released 2026).
- **Save model:** One island per Switch system (not per account), stored locally. Cloud save support added July 2020 after player outcry. The single-island restriction was a deliberate social design choice to encourage household collaboration.
- **Technical notes:** Undisclosed engine (Nintendo in-house). Runs at 1080p docked / 720p handheld at 30fps. No significant performance issues reported.
- **Live service:** Free updates through November 2021 added swimming, new villagers, Brewster's café, and farming. No paid content until the Happy Home Paradise DLC (Nov 2021, $24.99).

## Why it matters

ACNH demonstrated that a **soft narrative goal** (KK Slider concert) can give an otherwise open-ended life sim a satisfying arc without mandatory completion — the game never forces the rating grind, but the payoff (terraforming unlocks) is attractive enough that most players pursue it. The **real-time clock** is the defining cozy engagement hook: the world is always running, making the player feel like an active inhabitant rather than a god managing a simulation. The **dual-track economy** (Bells = spend, Nook Miles = try everything) is an elegant onboarding tool that rewards experimentation over pure optimization.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Soft story goal as engagement spine:** The KK Slider 3-star arc mirrors how Wayfinder could use the Trophy Chain — not as a hard gate but as an attractive milestone that unlocks new creative/gathering territory, then steps aside. See [[Trades and Leveling]] and [[Balance Philosophy]].
- **Real-time as cozy hook:** ACNH's world-persists-without-you feeling is worth considering for Wayfinder's session pacing; even without a real clock, the seasonal Affix calendar on Charts could create a similar "what's available this run" freshness — see [[Gathering]] and [[Economy]].
- **DIY discovery loop:** Finding new recipes via foraging, balloons, and NPCs is a low-friction discovery model for [[Crafting]]; Wayfinder's ink formula unlocks could use similar scattered-discovery beats rather than flat vendor purchase.
