# Tavern Keeper

Fantasy management sim (2025 Early Access, Greenheart Games) — the creators of Game Dev Tycoon spent 11 years building a cosy-chaotic tavern tycoon in which players construct, staff, stock, and narrate their way across a fantasy world while a freeform furniture sandbox lets them design every stick of furniture from scratch.

## Design

- **Cosy-chaos management loop:** Players build a tavern, hire staff, manage inventory and supplies, and react to emergent system interactions — dirt, heat, noise, flammability, and sickness collide to create unpredictable scenarios. The chaos is tunable: a Challenging mode demands tight optimization while a Relaxed mode removes pressure entirely.
- **Freeform decoration as a primary pillar:** Thousands of placeable items can be scaled, rotated, clipped, and recoloured; a Design Mode lets players craft entirely custom furniture from primitive shapes and share creations via a workshop. Decoration is not cosmetic — layout affects NPC pathing, comfort ratings, and customer spend.
- **Narrative campaign:** ~100 bite-sized stories narrated in full by audiobook actor Steven Pacey span multiple regions with distinct cultures (immortal elementals, halfling philosophers, orc inventors). The campaign delivers context for each new tavern location rather than treating each map as a blank sandbox.
- **Scalable scope:** Progression moves from a small roadside inn to a sprawling empire. The game explicitly supports both players who want to optimise every staff salary and those who only want to decorate.

## Implementation

- **Development duration:** Begun in 2014, the project changed game engines at least three times over eleven years before reaching Steam Early Access in November 2025 — one of the longest-documented indie development arcs for a management sim.
- **Small team, big scope:** Greenheart Games is a small independent studio best known for Game Dev Tycoon (2012, Greenlight era). Tavern Keeper represents a dramatic scope increase: the furniture sandbox alone required a procedural item-placement system flexible enough to support player-shared creations.
- **Early Access health:** Launched to 94% positive Steam reviews (2,779 English reviews) — rare for a complex management sim on day one.

## Why it matters

Tavern Keeper demonstrates that cosy and crunchy are not opposites: its adjustable difficulty means the same underlying simulation can read as a relaxed decoration toy or a tight efficiency puzzle. The freeform furniture system also shows that **player-authored spaces** can be a meaningful content layer — players create and share taverns, generating community content that extends replayability at no extra development cost. The 11-year journey and multiple engine switches are a cautionary tale about scope creep in management sims, but the 94% launch reception suggests iterating until the feel is right pays off.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Cosy-chaos tension as a design dial:** Tavern Keeper's Challenging/Relaxed toggle is a pattern worth considering for Wayfinder's dungeon difficulty — the same chart loop can read as a cozy skilling trip or a tense resource race depending on Affix selection; see [[Balance Philosophy]] and [[Affixes]].
- **Shared player-authored content:** The workshop furniture system mirrors how Wayfinder Charts (parameterized dungeon keys) could eventually be shared between players — player-inscribed charts as community-generated content; see [[Inks]] and [[The Crafting Bench]].
- **NPC ecosystem as economy driver:** Tavern patrons represent a captive NPC economy. Wayfinder's town NPCs should similarly create demand signals that shape what the player gathers and crafts; see [[Economy]] and [[NPCs]].
