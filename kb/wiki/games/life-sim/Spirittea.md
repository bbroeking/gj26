# Spirittea

Life sim / bathhouse management (November 2023, Cheesemaster Games / No More Robots) — Canadian solo developer Dan Beckerton fused Spirited Away's bathhouse concept with Stardew Valley's NPC social layer, adding a spirit-hunting detective loop that lets players collect and recruit supernatural patrons for their bathhouse.

## Design

- **Bathhouse management as the central income loop:** Players inherit a crumbling spirit bathhouse and restore it by chopping wood to heat baths, washing towels, managing water temperature, seating spirits beside companions they enjoy, and serving food. Unlike Stardew Valley's passive crop cycle, bathhouse income requires active service sessions — players choose when to open (they control day length and sleep timing), lending agency over pacing.
- **Spirit-vision and haunting investigation:** Drinking the eponymous Spirittea grants the ability to see spirits invisible to townsfolk. When a spirit causes a disturbance in town, players investigate — finding clues, solving the source — then witness a "trippy transformation" that reveals the spirit's true form before recruiting them as a bathhouse patron. Reviewers likened this to a Pokémon-esque collection mechanic with a detective wrapper.
- **NPC relationship layer:** 24 human townsfolk have daily routines, hobby preferences (fishing, karaoke, treasure hunting, BBQ, hot springs), and personal storylines. Befriending them unlocks shared activities during their free time. The developer Beckerton deliberately designed the "end goal" as simply finishing an aspiring author's book — no mandatory task lists, relationships are optional depth.
- **50/50 split philosophy:** Beckerton designed the game with equal weight on bathhouse operations (income and spirit management) and town social exploration, believing both halves would sustain player engagement across a 40–60 hour playthrough.
- **Inspirations:** Spirited Away (bathhouse setting), Natsume's Book of Friends (spirit encounter mechanics), Barakamon anime (rural relocation narrative), Earthbound and Harvest Moon (visual aesthetic).

## Implementation

- **Engine:** GameMaker Studio 2. Beckerton started in Unity but switched early, finding GMS2 better suited to the pixel-art life sim he wanted to build; prior experience from his first game Fables of Talumos (2019, also GMS2) shortened the learning curve significantly.
- **Solo development:** Beckerton built Spirittea alone, living off savings in Vancouver. He launched a Kickstarter in January 2020 (four months into production), which initially struggled but succeeded via high-tier pledges, ultimately attracting No More Robots publishing support.
- **Game Pass launch:** Spirittea released day-one on Xbox Game Pass, significantly expanding reach for a solo-dev pixel-art life sim that might otherwise struggle for visibility.

## Why it matters

Spirittea shows that the life-sim NPC loop can carry a **service management game** — the bathhouse is more dynamic than a farm because it demands reactive real-time choices (temperature, seating, towels) rather than just time-scheduled planting. The haunting investigation mechanic also demonstrates how a genre-adjacent hook (spirit detection = lite detective game) can motivate exploration without adding a full second game, keeping scope manageable for a solo developer. Its 50/50 time split between income and social layers is a replicable pacing model.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Service loop as income anchor:** The bathhouse open/close decision (player-controlled pacing, active service session) models how Wayfinder's [[NPCs]] could generate income demands — a craftsperson NPC needing supplies creates pull-based gather motivation rather than push-based task lists; see [[Economy]] and [[Gathering]].
- **Collection mechanic via investigation:** Spirit-hunting's clue-solve-recruit loop is a transferable pattern for Wayfinder's trophy chain — boss trophies unlock deeper dens the same way recruited spirits unlock new bathhouse capacity; see [[Balance Philosophy]].
- **Optional depth without mandatory grind:** Beckerton's "end goal = finish the book" philosophy — all social content is optional enrichment — maps to Wayfinder's cozy spine: the chart loop is the spine, dungeon combat is one verb, skilling is the heart; players who ignore the social NPC layer still have a complete game; see [[NPCs]] and [[Trades and Leveling]].
