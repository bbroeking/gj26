# Dinkum

Life sim / town-builder (EA July 2022 / 1.0 April 2025, James Bendon / Krafton) — Queensland solo developer James Bendon spent eight years teaching himself 3D modelling and programming to build an Animal Crossing–style town-builder set in Australia's red-soil outback, selling over one million copies by September 2025.

## Design

- **Gathering-first loop:** All progression flows from resource collection — farming (tilling, planting, harvesting), mining (Shiny Stones, ores), fishing, and bug-catching. The randomly generated island spans four biomes (bushland, tropical, desert, cold), each with distinct resource pools that require exploration to exploit.
- **Permit Points license system:** Progression gates are not gold but Permit Points — a secondary currency earned through milestones (distance walked, bugs caught, museum donations, bulletin board tasks, NPC daily jobs). Points buy Licenses from the NPC Fletch, which unlock better tools and crafting categories. Spending Dinks (gold equivalent) at the bank can also convert to Permit Points, linking the two currencies.
- **Town-building via Deeds and Debt:** Players unlock structures by acquiring a Deed, placing it, providing construction materials from gathered resources, and paying off the resulting Debt before the next Deed can be issued. This one-debt-at-a-time constraint paces town expansion and ensures every new building feels earned rather than bought.
- **Natural world design philosophy:** Bendon deliberately avoided "video gamey" abstractions — shops display items on tables rather than in menus; NPCs move through the world believably. He cited Animal Crossing's tactile interactions as the inspiration for keeping moment-to-moment actions grounded and legible.
- **Energy and food loop:** An energy bar depletes with activity; food and sleep restore it. The bar forces daily prioritization between gather types, creating natural variety without prescribing a routine.

## Implementation

- **Engine:** Unity.
- **Solo development:** Bendon began in November 2017, teaching himself programming and 3D modelling from scratch while working retail. He quit his job to focus full-time, developing for eight years before the April 2025 1.0 release. Early Access launched in July 2022, providing a sustained community and revenue stream during final development.
- **Publisher:** Krafton (PUBG parent company) acquired publishing rights, providing distribution but leaving development under Bendon's full creative control.
- **Commercial result:** 1 million units sold by September 2025; generally positive critical reception for environmental design; criticism for lack of Indigenous Australian representation given the setting's heavy use of Australian landscape signifiers.

## Why it matters

Dinkum proves that the Animal Crossing town-builder template can be extracted into a more systems-heavy survival-lite wrapper without losing the cosy appeal, and that a solo developer can execute that in 3D on Unity over a multi-year arc with commercial success. The **Permit Points license system** is a clean solution to the "too much gold, nothing to spend it on" mid-game economy problem: a second currency tied to activity breadth (not depth) encourages players to vary their gathering rather than grinding one resource to the exclusion of all others.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Permit Points as breadth incentive:** Dinkum's milestone-based Permit Points reward variety of activity (fish, mine, farm, donate) rather than volume of a single activity. Wayfinder's [[Trades and Leveling]] could adopt an analogous breadth incentive — bonus XP or Ink reagents for varying GatherNode kinds across a session; see [[Gathering]] and [[Economy]].
- **Debt-gated town expansion:** The one-Deed-one-Debt constraint that paces Dinkum's town growth is a pacing model for how Wayfinder NPC unlocks could be structured — each new [[NPCs|craftsperson NPC]] requiring a material investment that must be "paid off" before the next arrival, giving the player a concrete mid-term goal at all times.
- **Natural world legibility:** Bendon's rejection of menu-driven abstraction in favour of world-placed objects is a UI philosophy directly applicable to Wayfinder's cozy skilling spine — [[Gathering]] nodes, [[The Crafting Bench]], and NPC shops should feel discoverable in the world, not buried in panel menus; see [[NPCs]] and [[Balance Philosophy]].
