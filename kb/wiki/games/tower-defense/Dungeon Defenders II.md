# Dungeon Defenders II

Chromatic Games' free-to-play action-RPG/tower-defense hybrid (released 2017) in which four distinct hero classes each bring non-overlapping tower toolkits, making co-op composition a prerequisite for a complete defense rather than a multiplier on a single build.

## Design

The TD loop has a build phase and a combat phase. In the build phase, players spend mana (earned each wave) to place defenses — towers, traps, auras — unique to their chosen hero. The Apprentice deploys magical turrets; the Squire builds physical barricades; the Huntress lays traps; the Monk projects aura fields that buff allies or debuff enemies. These toolkits do not overlap: no single hero can construct a complete defence, so solo play requires juggling several heroes between waves while co-op assigns roles naturally. When waves begin, players fight directly alongside their defenses in third-person action combat.

The RPG layer runs deep. Loot (weapons, armour, accessories) drops from enemies and bosses, with stat rolls and reforge systems that drive character power over time. Hero levelling is per-character rather than account-level. Pets provide passive bonuses and level independently. This turns individual runs into both tactical puzzles (build the right towers) and loot optimisation sessions (hunt specific item stats), creating two reward loops that pull players through the same content.

Difficulty escalates over five rounds per map, with optional Incursion modes that add wave modifiers (additional enemy properties, environmental hazards) analogous to affix layers. Boss encounters cap map sequences with high-HP encounters requiring both tower support and active player DPS.

Free-to-play monetisation centres on hero unlocks and cosmetics; some reviewers note this creates friction in co-op when players cannot access the same class roster.

## Implementation

Developed by Chromatic Games; free-to-play on PC (Steam, 2017). Up to 4-player online co-op. Difficulty scales with player count. Engine is not publicly documented but the game uses Unreal-style visuals and is cross-platform PC/console. The original *Dungeon Defenders* (2011) established the formula on Unreal Engine 3; DD2 continued on an updated codebase.

## Why it matters

Dungeon Defenders II is the clearest example of hero-class TD: making each class architecturally responsible for a different defense layer forces genuine interdependence rather than parallel speedrunning. Combined with deep loot progression, it shows how a TD game can sustain a long tail of play — runs remain meaningful as loot grind long after players have solved the build puzzle.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

1. [[Multiplayer Co-op]] — Non-overlapping hero tower toolkits are a model for Wayfinder's Trade system in co-op: a Wayfinder (cartographer) and an Earthcraft player should contribute non-redundant dungeon capabilities, not just more of the same damage.
2. [[Economy]] — Mana-as-build-currency earned per wave maps onto Wayfinder's in-run resource economy; the rate at which mana refills shapes how many towers a team can maintain, directly analogous to how gather-node income might fund consumable deployment in dungeon runs.
3. [[Affixes]] — Incursion wave modifiers are a direct affix analogue: named per-map hazards and enemy properties that shift the optimal tower composition and demand specific hero pairings.
