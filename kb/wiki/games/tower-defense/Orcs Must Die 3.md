# Orcs Must Die 3

Robot Entertainment's 2021 action-tower-defense hybrid in which the player is a fully mobile combat unit placing traps between waves, making trap combo design and personal combat skill equally load-bearing.

## Design

The TD loop has two interleaved phases: a prep window to place and upgrade traps along corridors, then a wave in which the player jumps in with weapons and spells to supplement the trap kill-zone. Neither phase is passive — poor trap placement creates holes the player must plug personally, while ignoring direct combat costs resources that fund better traps. This makes spatial design feel like authorship rather than setup.

Trap types form a combo vocabulary. Slowing traps (oil slicks, tar) reduce enemy movement speed between damage traps; knockback traps (fans, push plates) redirect orcs into kill corridors or off ledges; damage traps (floor spikes, ceiling lasers, fire vents) deal damage only while enemies are in range. Combining types — e.g., oil pit into spinning blade into ledge — creates chained kill zones where cooldown management determines throughput. Fast enemies can trigger traps without being caught, requiring defenders to think about trap spacing and angle, not just damage per second.

The meta-progression layer is the skull system: each level awards skulls based on completion score, which fund trap and weapon upgrades in an inter-mission skill tree. Crucially, skulls are fully re-investable — players can undo upgrade paths between runs, enabling iteration without permanent punishment. A single dominant combo exists (confirmed by reviewers) that can carry the campaign, suggesting the system values creative expression over enforced variety.

War Scenarios are the standout mode: oversized maps defend against 1,000-unit armies, unlocking macro-scale War Machines (catapults, archer battalions, giant floor panels) unavailable elsewhere. These function as a distinct sub-game with wider tactical vocabulary.

## Implementation

Built on Unreal Engine 4 by Robot Entertainment. 2-player online co-op throughout; co-op amplifies trap-coverage debate (who covers which corridor?) and enables role-splitting (trapper vs. fighter). Originally a Stadia exclusive, released for PC and consoles in 2021. Metacritic: 73 (Stadia) / 77 (PC).

## Why it matters

OMD3 is the genre's clearest proof that making the player a first-class combat unit does not dilute strategic trap placement — it sharpens it by making every failure attributable. The skull re-spec system is a model for meta-progression that invites experimentation rather than taxing mistakes, highly applicable to any roguelike-adjacent loop.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

1. [[Combat]] — The player-as-unit model maps directly onto Wayfinder's "combat as one verb" design; how OMD3 balances personal damage contribution against passive trap output is directly analogous to how Wayfinder's chart runs should weight active player skill.
2. [[Affixes]] — Wave-by-wave enemy composition variations (fast small orcs, shielded brutes, flying units) that demand specific trap responses are equivalent to Wayfinder's affix-shaped encounter design.
3. [[Economy]] — The skull re-spec system is a template for Wayfinder's inter-run upgrade loop: spend resources earned from runs, iterate without permanent lock-in.
