# Sanctum 2

Coffee Stain Studios' 2013 FPS/tower-defense hybrid in which players construct maze-like tower corridors during a prep phase, then drop into first-person to personally fight alongside them when waves begin — proving the two genres are complementary rather than competing.

## Design

The TD loop alternates between two distinct phases. Build phase: players spend resource pickups to place tower blocks and walls, constructing mazes that funnel alien Krilons through maximum-damage corridors. Wave phase: the first-person perspective unlocks and players become active combatants — guns, grenades, and movement are as important to the defense as tower placement. Unlike Orcs Must Die (third-person), Sanctum 2's FPS framing makes the combat phase feel kinematically familiar to a shooter audience, lowering the genre barrier.

Resource pickup placement rewards exploration: materials are scattered across the map between waves, meaning players who patrol gather more build currency, creating a second loop within the prep phase. Tower variety covers slowing, crowd control, and burst damage; walls are buildable to extend corridors, so the maze layout is genuinely freeform rather than snap-to-grid. Planning a maze with teammates becomes a negotiation over which approaches are worth defending and who controls which towers.

The perk and loadout system adds depth between runs: players choose a tower set, a weapon set, and a perk set before each map, so two players with different loadouts bring genuinely non-overlapping defensive tools. Four character classes each carry unique weapons and innate bonuses. A visual novel sidebar (unlockable comic pages per completed map) delivers lore in a way that doesn't interrupt the TD loop.

Co-op tension: because resource pickup is free-for-all, a teammate who over-spends early can starve the rest of the team. Difficulty scaling accounts for player count, but co-ordination breakdowns are identified by reviewers as the game's primary failure mode, suggesting the resource model rewards communication more than it enforces it mechanically.

## Implementation

Developed by Coffee Stain Studios (Sweden) in Unreal Engine 3; released May 2013 on PC, later PS3. Up to 4-player online co-op throughout the campaign. Very positive Steam reception (92% of 4,300+ reviews). Single-player is available but not recommended by the developer — the co-op balance is assumed.

## Why it matters

Sanctum 2 is the genre's strongest case that active personal combat during waves raises, not lowers, the strategic ceiling of tower placement: if the player can cover gaps directly, each tower placed must earn its cost against that alternative. It also demonstrates that FPS mobility and tower-defense planning occupy different cognitive modes that can coexist without either feeling watered-down.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

1. [[Combat]] — The "personal combat as gap-plugging" model is the closest existing reference to Wayfinder's "combat as one verb" framing: Sanctum 2 proves the active player doesn't trivialise tower-equivalent defenses if both are priced correctly.
2. [[Multiplayer Co-op]] — Free-for-all resource pickup creates organic communication pressure without enforcing explicit role assignment; Wayfinder could borrow this for gather-node competition within dungeon co-op runs.
3. [[Economy]] — Loadout selection before a map (tower set, weapon set, perk set) maps directly to Wayfinder's pre-run chart-prep phase: players commit a build before seeing the floor.
