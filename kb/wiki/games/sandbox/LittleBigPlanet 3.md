# LittleBigPlanet 3

The third entry in Sony's flagship UGC platformer series, developed by Sumo Digital (2014, PS3/PS4), which expanded creation depth to 16 layers, 70 tools, and multi-level Adventures — before Sony shut down its servers in April 2024, permanently destroying access to over 10 million user-made levels without prior warning.

## Design

**Creation tools.** LBP3 substantially upgraded the prior series formula. Levels now use **16 depth layers** (up from three in LBP1/2), enabling genuine 3D-feeling 2D stages. A **Dynamic Thermometer** system streams objects in and out based on proximity, allowing far denser scenes than the hardware's fixed RAM limit would otherwise permit. The **70-tool toolkit** covers logic wiring (circuit boards), material brushes, decoration stickers, sound effects, and the new *Adventures* format — a multi-level story container with shared progression state, closer to a mini-game than a single level.

**Discoverability.** Levels were browsed via the Community Moon: a searchable, tag-filtered hub showing rating counts, play counts, and the Mm heart (Media Molecule endorsement). The browse interface was notoriously clunky — keyword search with limited faceting — but sheer volume (tens of millions of levels across the series) meant top-rated content surfaced naturally.

**Social.** Up to four players could cooperate in story mode or custom levels. LBP3 introduced four distinct characters with different traversal abilities (wall-jump, flight, size-shift), encouraging creators to design multi-character puzzles. Level linking let creators chain stages into serialised campaigns.

**Standout mechanic.** The **Play + Create + Share** loop — begin as a player; hold Square to enter Create Mode anywhere in the Community Moon; publish with one button — collapsed the creator/consumer barrier in a way few games before it had. Creating was free; sharing was instant; playing was the reward.

## Implementation

Developed by **Sumo Digital** with Media Molecule and Sony XDev, LBP3 shipped November 2014 on **PlayStation 3 and PlayStation 4**. All community content lived on Sony servers; no local backup pathway was officially provided. On **January 8, 2024**, the PS4 servers were taken offline "temporarily" due to technical issues. On **April 19, 2024**, Sony confirmed the shutdown was permanent. Locally saved levels remained playable offline; all server-stored content was lost. The game was delisted from the PlayStation Store on **October 31, 2024**.

## Why it matters

LBP3's server shutdown is a landmark case in **game preservation and UGC fragility**. At least 10 million user-created levels vanished permanently, with no warning period for community preservation efforts. The disaster illustrates the structural risk of hosting UGC exclusively on first-party servers with no archival commitment — a lesson increasingly cited in discussions of platform responsibility. The loss also closed the LittleBigPlanet series: PS3 servers had already gone offline, and no successor has been announced. For UGC platforms, LBP3 is now a cautionary template: *if you own the content, you are the single point of failure.*

## Relevance to Wayfinder

1. **[[Economy]]** — The total loss of creator-made content when servers shut down is a direct warning about Wayfinder chart storage: charts (dungeon keys) should either be locally exportable or archivable, not purely server-resident.
2. **[[Multiplayer Co-op]]** — LBP3's four-character co-op with asymmetric abilities is a strong reference for co-op dungeon design where classes serve different traversal and combat roles.
3. **[[Design Influences]]** — The Play + Create + Share loop that collapses the creator/player divide is worth revisiting when designing Wayfinder's chart-inscription craft — can chart-makers feel like players?
