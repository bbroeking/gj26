# Workers and Resources Soviet Republic

Cold War-era city-building and logistics simulation (Early Access 2019, full release June 2024, 3Division / Hooded Horse) set in an Eastern Bloc republic during the 1960s–1990s, distinguished by the deepest transport and supply-chain simulation in the colony-sim genre — including optional Realistic Mode that removes all abstraction from construction and procurement.

## Design

- **Transport network as the primary system.** Over 30 commodities — including ores, refined metals, concrete, food, fuel, chemicals, and consumer goods — each require specific vehicle and storage types. Liquids move through pipe networks or tanker trucks; bulk ore travels by conveyor belt, flatbed train car, or dump truck; prefab construction panels require flatbed transport. The player designs and staffs every vehicle line, manages fuel depots, and schedules maintenance. No logistics is abstracted away.
- **Construction pipeline in Realistic Mode.** The game's default mode allows instant building purchase; Realistic Mode eliminates this entirely. Every structure must be built by a construction crew that physically hauls materials (concrete, steel beams, gravel, prefab panels) from storage to the site. Buildings also cannot be demolished for free — demolition produces rubble that must itself be transported. This converts city-building from a financial simulation into a physical one.
- **Dual-currency import economy.** Resources unavailable domestically can be imported via two customs houses: Eastern bloc goods cost rubles (the internal economy currency); Western technology costs dollars (earned only through exports). This encodes Cold War economic friction into the game mechanic — acquiring Western technology requires building a viable export economy first, creating a long-range planning horizon.
- **Citizen health and happiness as production inputs.** Workers require housing, food, healthcare, and cultural amenities to maintain productivity. Unlike simpler sims where happiness is a separate score, W&R:SR workers who are unhealthy or miserable simply do not show up to jobs, creating immediate production failures — the connection between citizen welfare and industrial output is direct and consequential.
- **Standout mechanic — the routing problem.** Every vehicle line must be manually configured: route, number of vehicles, stop order, cargo type, loading and unloading points. A steel mill that runs short because a truck line is under-staffed is not a game event — it is a player error in logistics design. The game trains players to think in throughput and bottlenecks rather than building counts.

## Implementation

- Developed in a proprietary engine by 3Division, a small Slovak studio. The transport simulation runs vehicles as individual agents with fuel consumption, weight capacity, and speed modelled; road gradient affects vehicle performance.
- Published by Hooded Horse (the same publisher behind Manor Lords), signalling the "hardcore city-builder" niche as a viable commercial category.
- Five-year Early Access (2019–2024); three DLC expansions: Biomes (June 2024), World Maps (December 2024), Early Start (May 2025). The extended early access allowed deep community-driven balancing of the logistics simulation.
- Mostly Positive Steam reviews across tens of thousands of ratings. Praised for simulation depth; criticised for steep learning curve and sparse early tutorials.

## Why it matters

Workers and Resources: Soviet Republic is the **existence proof that players will engage with logistics simulation at a granularity that rivals industrial engineering software**, provided the framing (Cold War nation-building) supplies sufficient narrative context. Realistic Mode in particular demonstrates that removing abstraction can *increase* engagement rather than decrease it — when every delivery matters, players become invested in their transport networks the way factory managers become invested in their supply chains.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- The **physical construction pipeline** (materials must be transported to build sites) is a north-star reference for [[Gathering]] depth: in a more ambitious version of Wayfinder, Gathered materials could have explicit transport legs between gather node, satchel, town storage, and crafting bench — each leg a potential failure point.
- The **dual-currency export loop** (ruble/dollar split) maps onto Wayfinder's intended [[Economy]]: chart profits in common currency vs. rare trophy-gated unlocks creates a two-speed economy with meaningful specialisation decisions.
- The **throughput mindset** — routing and bottleneck analysis — is transferable to [[Crafting]] recipe design: recipes should have choke points (rare reagents, time-limited channels) so players feel the satisfaction of clearing a bottleneck rather than just clicking a crafting button.
