# Anno 1800

Industrial-era city-builder strategy game (2019, Ubisoft Blue Byte) set during the 19th-century Industrial Revolution, renowned for its interlocking multi-tier production chains, dual-world island colonisation, and citizen-need satisfaction as the engine of progression.

## Design

- **Population tier system as gated progression.** Citizens advance through five residential tiers (Farmers → Workers → Artisans → Engineers → Investors), each unlocking new buildings and more complex production chains. Crucially, higher-tier citizens cannot substitute for lower-tier labour: Farmers are permanently required for agricultural buildings regardless of city advancement. Lower-tier population never becomes obsolete — the city must maintain a working-class base even as it builds an investor class on top.
- **Production chain complexity as the core loop.** Chains range from simple one-step recipes to multi-branch networks: sewing machines require steel (iron → steel furnace) plus wooden frames (lumber → carpentry) plus sewing needles (iron → needle workshop), each on different production cycle timings. Players must calculate factory ratios and balance throughput across dozens of parallel chains simultaneously, a form of industrial logistics optimization.
- **Dual-world island system.** The Old World (temperate Europe) and New World (tropical Americas) produce complementary goods: cotton, tobacco, and chocolate originate in the New World and must be transported via trade route to satisfy Old World artisan/engineer needs. This forces players to maintain an inter-continental economy, adding a global logistics layer atop local city planning.
- **Industrialisation trade-off.** Factory construction reduces city attractiveness, driving down tourism revenue. Players must spatially segregate industrial zones from parks and cultural buildings, creating urban zoning decisions with economic consequences — a subtle simulation of the social costs of industrialisation.
- **Standout mechanic — blueprint mode.** Players can lay out planned buildings in blueprint ghost mode without spending resources, enabling city planning ahead of resource availability. This transforms city design from reactive improvisation into deliberate architecture, reducing friction for players who want to plan before they build.

## Implementation

- Proprietary Ubisoft Blue Byte engine; DirectX 11 on PC; later ported to PS5/Xbox Series X|S (2023).
- Production chain logic is table-driven rather than simulated agent-by-agent; factories produce goods at fixed intervals, and output flows through road/dock networks to warehouses. The apparent complexity emerges from chain depth, not from agent simulation.
- Commercial success: fastest-selling Anno game, selling four times more copies than Anno 2205 in its first week. Supported by four seasonal DLC passes adding arctic exploration, skyscrapers, airships, and new population tiers through 2023.
- Metacritic: 78–83 across platforms.

## Why it matters

Anno 1800 is the benchmark for **production chain design in city-builders**: its tier system ensures that progression never deprecates previous investments, its dual-world structure forces players to think in inter-regional trade rather than self-sufficiency, and its blueprint mode established a standard that later builders adopted. The game demonstrates that supply-chain optimization can be as engaging as combat — given sufficient chain depth and legible visualisation.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- The **never-obsolete lower tier** principle applies directly to [[Crafting]]: basic gathered materials (ore, forage) should remain relevant inputs to advanced recipes, so Earthcraft levelling never renders early gathering pointless.
- The **dual-world logistics** (two zones with complementary outputs) maps onto Wayfinder's intended [[Economy]]: the Overworld and crypt dungeons produce different resource categories that only combine in town crafting, creating a reason to delve rather than grind the surface.
- The **blueprint planning mode** suggests a UI pattern for [[Gathering]] route-planning or chart pre-inscription: letting players sketch a chart's affix configuration before spending Ink creates deliberate investment moments.
