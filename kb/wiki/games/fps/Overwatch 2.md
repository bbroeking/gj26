# Overwatch 2

Overwatch 2 is a 2022 free-to-play live-service hero shooter by Blizzard Entertainment that replaced the original Overwatch, pivoting from loot boxes to a seasonal battle-pass model and igniting a sustained debate about whether monetization-gated heroes undermine game balance and player trust.

## Design

- **6v6 → 5v5 shift.** The most structural design change: eliminating one tank per side was intended to speed up play and reduce the "tank bunker" meta. Controversial — the community debated for years whether this produced faster or merely more chaotic games.
- **Seasonal model.** Nine-week seasons, each delivering a new hero or map on alternating cycles. Balance patches anchored to season starts. This cadence kept the game visible in streaming/media while reducing the previous "big patch" unpredictability.
- **Battle-pass hero lock (Season 1–9).** New heroes launched behind the premium battle pass tier, which generated immediate, sustained backlash. Players argued that locking heroes affected competitive integrity, not just cosmetics.
- **Season 10 reversal.** Blizzard made all new heroes permanently free-to-unlock starting Season 10 (April 2024) — a public acknowledgement that the hero-lock had been a design and PR mistake. Loot boxes also returned (February 2025) with guaranteed rarity floors, closing a circle begun in 2022.

## Implementation

- Built on the same Prometheus engine as [[Overwatch]], with network and rendering improvements for the new seasonal cadence. Cross-play and cross-progression introduced across all platforms for the free-to-play launch.
- The Steam launch (August 2023) quickly became the platform's worst-rated title — largely driven by protest reviews from Chinese players following NetEase server closures rather than gameplay complaints, illustrating how platform review systems can conflate political events with design feedback.
- Director Jeff Kaplan's April 2021 departure, attributed to internal pressure around monetization targets, became a public signal of the commercial-vs-design tension inside the project.

## Why it matters

Overwatch 2 is the clearest case study in the live-service pivot trap: abandoning a working premium model mid-lifecycle to chase recurring revenue, then being forced to roll back the most contentious features under player pressure. The hero-lock reversal shows that competitive games have a higher tolerance ceiling for cosmetic monetization and a much lower ceiling for gameplay-access monetization. The 5v5 change illustrates how structural rule changes in live games carry enormous risk — the player base had nine years of 6v6 muscle memory.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Monetization lesson.** Lock cosmetics, never progression or roster access — see [[MMO Progression Systems]]. Wayfinder's chart/economy design should keep power-unlocking strictly in-game.
- **Seasonal content cadence.** The nine-week rhythm is a useful anchor for planning Wayfinder's future live content intervals — see [[MMO Social and Endgame]].
- **Public trust.** Blizzard's rocky reception shows that players read monetization decisions as a signal of intent; early transparency about the economy model matters — relevant to [[Balance Philosophy]].
