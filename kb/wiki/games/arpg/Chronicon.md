# Chronicon

A pixel-art isometric ARPG (Early Access 2015, full release 2020) by two-person Swedish studio Subworld, offering one of the genre's deepest itemization systems — over 900 skills and 700+ randomized items — at a fraction of the development budget of its AAA peers.

## Design

**Combat and classes.** Four classes — Templar, Berserker, Warden, and Warlock — each possess four skill trees that can be mixed and matched freely within the class. With over 900 total skills, abilities, and perks across the roster, Chronicon's depth rivals [[Path of Exile]] in concept if not in raw breadth. Each class plays distinctly: Templar is tanky and holy-damage-focused, Berserker melee-frenzied, Warden a nature/pet hybrid, Warlock a curse-and-summon caster. Builds emerge from the intersection of skill-tree choices, gear affixes, and crafted modifications.

**Itemization.** The item system is the game's headline feature. Over 400 unique items with fixed special powers sit alongside 700+ base items with randomized enchantment rolls and quality tiers. Crafting is layered: enchanting (strengthen or reroll existing affixes), gem insertion, empowering (raise item power directly), and rune inscription. Unique and legendary items carry extractable power modifiers — a player can harvest a mod from a dropped item and graft it onto their preferred gear piece (once), allowing targeted build completion without pure RNG dependency. Sorting through and crafting toward an optimized setup is explicitly the primary pleasure of the game.

**Endgame: Anomalies and Mastery.** The campaign spans five acts; endgame centers on Anomalies — randomly generated dungeons accessible without keys, maps, or gatekeeping resources. Players simply run Anomalies as many times as desired. A Mastery system provides infinite character progression beyond the level cap: each Mastery rank awards a Mastery Point spendable on a secondary passive layer with no ceiling. This mirrors [[Diablo III]]'s Paragon system but is more explicit about its infinity; Chronicon does not pretend the Mastery system ends.

**Standout mechanic.** The uncapped Mastery + free Anomaly access is a deliberate statement: Chronicon removes the friction tokens (keys, maps, emblems) that other ARPGs use to gate endgame sessions. The result is frictionless loop access — run when you want, as long as you want.

## Implementation

Developed by Daniel Stigsjöö (primary developer) and Marika Assgård at Subworld, near Gothenburg, Sweden. Built in a proprietary engine with pixel-art rendering. Five years in Early Access (2015–2020); the Early Access period was community-collaborative, with many player-suggested systems incorporated directly. Two DLC expansions followed the 1.0 launch: *Ancient Beasts* (February 2022) and *The Mechanist* (January 2023, adding a fifth class with jetpack mobility and gun/axe duality). Local co-op supports up to four players on one machine with Remote Play Together extending it online. No public matchmaking.

## Why it matters

Chronicon demonstrates that deep ARPG itemization can be built by two people with community partnership and a long Early Access arc. The removal of endgame friction tokens, combined with the crafting system's targeted-mod extraction, shows a philosophy of maximizing the time players spend in the pleasurable core loop (build-crafting, running dungeons) and minimizing time in gatekeeping logistics.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Chart Loop]] friction.** Chronicon's key-free Anomaly access is a useful counterpoint to [[Path of Exile]]'s Atlas gating; Wayfinder's Charts should feel closer to Chronicon — low barrier to *entering* a run, but the Chart *itself* encodes the session's challenges and rewards.
- **[[Items and Gear]] — extractable mods.** The ability to harvest a unique modifier from one item and transplant it to preferred gear is a player-empowering alternative to pure RNG; worth considering as a Chart trophy reward or Inscription mechanic.
- **[[Affixes]] depth at small scale.** Chronicon proves 700+ randomized item affix combinations are achievable with a tiny team; Wayfinder's item scope should target similar density without needing a large studio.
