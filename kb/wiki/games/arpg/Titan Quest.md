# Titan Quest

Mythology-themed action-RPG released June 2006 by Iron Lore Entertainment (Brian Sullivan, co-creator of Age of Empires) spanning Ancient Greece, Egypt, and Asia, pioneering the dual mastery class combination system that directly seeded [[Grim Dawn]] and the broader ARPG genre.

## Design

**Dual mastery system.** Players do not choose a fixed class at the start; the first level-up presents a pick from eight masteries (Defence, Warfare, Hunting, Rogue, Earth, Storm, Nature, Spirit). A second mastery unlocks later, generating 36 possible class combinations each with a proper class name — e.g., Warfare + Defence = Conqueror, Nature + Earth = Summoner, Storm + Spirit = Oracle. Single-mastery builds are valid but weaker, so the combinatorial choice is the game's core identity. Each mastery has a dedicated skill tree; points can be invested in individual skills (improving them) or in the mastery bar itself (providing passive attribute bonuses).

**Mythology and setting.** Three-act campaign traverses Greece → Egypt → Silk Road/China, drawing enemies from each civilization's mythology with creature designs inspired by Ray Harryhausen's stop-motion films (Clash of the Titans, Jason and the Argonauts). Storyteller NPCs offer lore recaps rather than quest-gating. The layered geography meant each act had a visually distinct enemy roster, preventing the "reskinned mob" fatigue common in single-biome ARPGs.

**Itemization.** Color-coded rarity tiers (white → yellow/magic → blue/rare → purple/legendary). Relics and Charms occupy a dedicated item slot — Orange rarity items providing elemental resistances and passive bonuses that stack with gear affixes. The *Immortal Throne* expansion (2007) added Artifacts (crafted from three components at an Enchanter NPC), a third gear layer beyond the base rarity tiers. Over 100,000 item combinations were cited by the developers.

**Co-op.** Up to seven players cooperatively, via LAN or internet. Loot is instanced by player.

**Expansions and revival.** Iron Lore closed in 2008 after a failed project post-Titan Quest. THQ Nordic acquired the IP and released the Anniversary Edition in 2016 (free upgrade, improved netcode, widescreen support) and three further expansions between 2017–2021 (*Ragnarök*, *Atlantis*, *Eternal Embers*). A full sequel, Titan Quest II, is in development (as of 2026).

## Implementation

Proprietary engine built from scratch — no adequate third-party engine existed at the time for the team's vision. Tile-based environment system with height-map terrain. Initial PC release June 2006. Metacritic 77/100. Revenue was modest but the game gained cult status, directly influencing the founding team of Crate Entertainment (several Iron Lore alumni) who built [[Grim Dawn]] on a modified Titan Quest engine.

## Why it matters

Titan Quest invented the class-combination-as-character-creation model that became the template for [[Grim Dawn]] and influenced countless ARPGs. The two-mastery pick gives players enormous build identity without requiring a traditional class system, and naming the resulting combination provides immediate legibility ("I'm a Conqueror" conveys a build archetype). The Relic/Charm slot anticipated the modern idea of a dedicated "build-modifier" slot distinct from primary gear.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Skills]] and Trade combinations.** The dual mastery naming convention — two systems cross-pollinating into a named archetype — is a model for how Wayfinder's [[Skills|Trade hotbar skills]] could develop named synergies when a player levels multiple Trades simultaneously (e.g., Wayfinder + Earthcraft = a chart-shaping Rootwarden build).
- **[[Items and Gear]] slot design.** Titan Quest's dedicated Relic/Charm slot (build-modifier outside the normal gear ladder) could inform a Wayfinder slot for chart-inscribed items or dungeon-found relics that modify player capability without requiring an entire gear replacement.
- **Mythology-as-biome structure.** Distinct enemy rosters per geographic/mythological biome (Greece / Egypt / Asia) maps directly to Wayfinder's planned biome structure (crypt / forest / etc.) — each biome should have a coherent creature mythology rather than scaled reskins.
