# Van Helsing Final Cut

A gothic-noir isometric ARPG compilation (2015, NeocoreGames) that stitches all three Van Helsing games into one unified campaign with six classes and the series' signature mechanic: an AI companion, Lady Katarina, who has her own build, inventory, and behavioral modes.

## Design

**Classes and skills.** Final Cut offers six character classes — a melee knight (Hunter), a stealth assassin, a versatile sniper, a warlock, a heavily armored soldier, and a turret-deploying robotic engineer. Each class has 24 skills, 12 auras, and three class-specific abilities, arranged in skill trees with a Rage-powered bonus layer: each skill has unlock-able power bonuses that activate when the player spends Rage (a third resource filled in combat), effectively adding a high-pressure "super" mode to individual abilities rather than a global ultimate.

**Katarina companion system.** Lady Katarina is the series' defining feature: a permanently present AI-controlled ghost companion with her own inventory, her own skill trees (one combat tree, one Sentinel passive tree), and behavioral mode switching. The player can command her to fight in either melee or ranged stance, or to enter Ghostly Form — invisible and invulnerable to enemies, unable to attack, but providing passive bonuses from the Sentinel tree. Katarina levels alongside the player but slightly slower. She accepts equipment and can be built as a second character, effectively making solo Van Helsing a pseudo-two-character experience.

**Tower defense integration.** At designated points in the campaign, the game shifts into an optional (but encouraged) tower defense mode: the player defends a location using placeable structures while Van Helsing and Katarina fight alongside the defenses. Reviewers consistently found these segments "a surprising amount of fun" and noted they break up the hack-and-slash pacing meaningfully.

**Itemization.** Loot volume is high, but stat increases dominate; truly build-defining unique items are sparse. The reviewer consensus is that itemization is the weakest system — "if it's stronger, use it" — and lacks the affix complexity of contemporaries. A crafting system exists but is described as limited in scope.

**Endgame: Scenario Mode.** After the campaign, Scenario Mode provides standalone missions and challenge layers for improved loot. The endgame is narrower than games like [[Diablo III]] or [[Path of Exile 2]] but sufficient to extend play for completionists.

**Standout mechanic.** Katarina's behavioral mode switching — combat mode vs. Sentinel-passive ghost form — is one of the most nuanced companion systems in the ARPG genre. The player effectively manages two build paths simultaneously, with situational mode-switching during combat serving as a micro-strategy layer.

## Implementation

Developed by Hungarian studio NeocoreGames on their proprietary Helsing 3 engine, which the studio credited with improved performance, faster load times, and higher frame rates compared to the original trilogy's engine. Final Cut was released November 6, 2015 for PC; console ports followed. Co-op supports up to four players online. NeocoreGames later applied their ARPG expertise to [[Warhammer Inquisitor Martyr]].

## Why it matters

Van Helsing Final Cut's Katarina system is a proof-of-concept that a richly designed NPC companion can carry a meaningful second build axis without requiring a second human player. For a cozy dungeon-crawler where solo play is common, the companion-as-pseudo-co-op model is directly applicable. The tower defense integration also demonstrates that hybrid genre segments can deepen the loop without breaking the ARPG feel if they share the same gear and skill systems.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Multiplayer Co-op]] and companion design.** Katarina's dual-mode system (combat vs. Sentinel passive) shows how a companion can provide meaningful strategic texture in solo play — relevant if Wayfinder ever adds a companion NPC to solo Chart runs.
- **[[Skills]] — Rage-powered bonuses per ability.** The per-skill Rage-unlock system is a compact "power state" model that avoids a single global ultimate, keeping individual skills feeling distinctive under pressure.
- **[[Chart Loop]] pacing.** The tower-defense interludes illustrate that brief mode shifts within a dungeon run (defend a node, escort an NPC) can refresh pacing without fragmenting identity — a potential Chart affix type for Wayfinder.
