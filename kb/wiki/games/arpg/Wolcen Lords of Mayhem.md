# Wolcen Lords of Mayhem

A visually ambitious classless ARPG (2020, Wolcen Studio) best known for its rotating concentric passive tree — the Gate of Fates — and a city-building endgame layer called Stormfall.

## Design

**Combat and build system.** Wolcen is classless: any combination of gear and skills is legal. Active skills are dropped as loot and can be slotted freely; each skill carries an "Affinity" alignment (Ferocity, Toughness, or Wisdom) that interacts with a three-way character stat. Skills are modified through attached modifiers that alter projectile count, area of effect, damage type, or behavior — a system that echoes [[Path of Exile]]'s gem-socket philosophy but is item-driven rather than socket-driven. Combat is fast, visually flashy, and built around dodge-rolling and a "Break Bar" on tougher enemies that staggers them when depleted.

**Gate of Fates.** The passive tree is three concentric rings — 3 inner segments, 6 middle, 12 outer — each holding clusters of passive nodes grouped into archetypes (Warmonger, Soldier of Misfortune, Plaguebringer, etc.). The defining mechanic: the player can *rotate each ring independently*, physically repositioning the connections between segments to open entirely new paths. This means two characters allocated into different regions can share nodes if the player rotates the rings to bridge them — creating builds that standard fixed-tree games cannot express. Respec costs credits, discouraging constant change but not locking decisions permanently.

**Itemization.** Gear drops on a power-level axis; uniques and set items carry modifiers that redefine skill behavior at extremes. Sockets allow gem insertion for further stat layering. Critics noted itemization math was "wonky at launch" — many numbers were badly balanced — but the design intent was deep: rare items were meant to pivot entire builds, not merely inflate damage numbers.

**Endgame: Stormfall.** After the campaign, Stormfall city becomes a buildable account-wide hub. Players spend Primordial Affinity (endgame currency) to construct and upgrade city buildings — armouries, academies, marketplaces — each of which unlocks services, passive bonuses, or new mission types. Endgame missions generate Stormfall currency and are scaled to item power. The system is account-level persistence rather than per-character, functioning as a shared metagame progression layer.

**Standout mechanic.** The rotating passive tree is one of the most original passive-progression systems in the genre. Rather than choosing a path through a static graph, the player chooses which graph to construct each session.

## Implementation

Developed by French studio Wolcen Studio using CryEngine, which gives the game its unusually high visual fidelity for the genre. Released February 2020 after five years in Early Access; the launch was critically marred by server outages and balance bugs, significantly damaging the initial reception. Post-launch patches addressed many issues. The game supports online co-op for up to four players.

## Why it matters

The Gate of Fates demonstrates that ARPG passive trees can carry mechanical originality beyond node placement — the *shape* of the tree and the player's ability to reshape it is itself a strategic layer. The Stormfall city system is a strong example of account-level endgame persistence that doesn't reset per character.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Chart Loop]] as a session key.** Wolcen's per-run city currency loop (run mission → earn Primordial Affinity → upgrade hub) mirrors the Chart-as-dungeon-key model: each run funds the next tier of access.
- **[[Affixes]] density vs. passive tree.** Gate of Fates rotation is a high-complexity analogue to Affix stacking: both let players construct increasingly specialized configurations without locking into a class identity.
- **[[Skills]] as loot.** Skill-as-droppable-item is a design Wayfinder may want to consider for expanding the hotbar roster without class restrictions.
