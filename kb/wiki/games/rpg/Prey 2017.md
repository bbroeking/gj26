# Prey 2017

First-person immersive sim (2017, Arkane Austin / Bethesda) set aboard space station Talos I, where the player absorbs alien and human abilities via Neuromods to survive a Typhon infestation in an interconnected mega-dungeon built for multi-solution play.

## Design

- **Neuromods as hybrid skill tree.** Abilities unlock along two parallel trees: human powers (hacking, leverage, stealth, passive buffs) and alien Typhon powers (telekinesis, psychic blasts, the ability to mimic inanimate objects). The split creates meaningful identity tension — leaning alien triggers turret hostility, a systemic consequence woven into the world rather than a morality label.
- **Multi-solution traversal.** Every space on Talos I was designed with at least three paths in mind: a locked door can be hacked, the wall beside it can be blown through, a GLOO-foam platform can reach a vent above, or an enemy can be Possessed to open it from the inside. The station functions as a Metroidvania, with acquired abilities permanently unlocking new routes through already-visited areas.
- **GLOO Cannon.** A tool-weapon that fires expanding foam: freezes enemies, creates climbable platforms on flat walls, and smothers fires. Its dual-use nature exemplifies Prey's "everything is a system" ethos — no item exists only to do one thing.
- **Mimic enemies.** Typhon Mimics disguise themselves as office chairs, mugs, and supply boxes, making every static prop a potential threat. This keeps players actively reading the environment rather than treating background art as filler.
- **Mooncrash DLC — roguelike on an immersive sim chassis.** Arkane layered roguelike randomization atop Talos I's intact level geometry: hazard channels (radiation, fire, flooding) randomly activate in different sections each run; five survivors with narrow skill trees can each be played and killed independently within a single session; a Corruption meter (inspired by Risk of Rain's difficulty scaling) pressures forward momentum. Lead designer Richard Wilson noted that "shorter loops allow players to remember previous choices," validating replayability without wholesale procedural generation.

## Implementation

- **CryEngine.** Arkane Austin chose CryEngine for its physics fidelity and large interconnected spaces, enabling the debris-scattered, zero-gravity exterior sections. The station's circular layout meant no hard loading screens between most areas.
- **"Meaningful variability" over full procedural generation** in Mooncrash: five distinct environmental hazard states were mixed and layered rather than generating novel geometry, avoiding the "chunkiness" of layouts that feel technically unique but experientially identical.
- Despite strong critical reception (retrospectively ranked among the best immersive sims ever made), disappointing commercial performance prompted Bethesda to redirect Arkane Austin toward live-service projects. Prey's sales failure is a cautionary data point for systemic games in a market that under-markets genre nuance.

## Why it matters

Prey is the clearest modern proof that **skill trees can be world-reactive systems, not just number columns**. The alien-power hostility mechanic is a masterclass in consequence-without-moralizing. Mooncrash demonstrates that immersive sim spaces can be reused as roguelike arenas if loop length is shortened and hazard states replace geometry randomization — a design pivot worth studying for any game that wants both deep systems and replayability.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Skills]] / [[Trades and Leveling]]:** The Neuromod dual-tree (human vs. alien, with world-systemic trade-offs) is a model for how Wayfinder's Trades could create meaningful identity tension — investing in one Trade changes how NPCs or world systems respond, not just which numbers go up.
- **[[Dungeon Generation]]:** Mooncrash's hazard-channel approach (overlay environmental states on fixed geometry instead of generating new geometry) is directly applicable to Wayfinder's crypt runs: affix-driven hazard states (cursed air, flooded floors, rampant thorns) recontextualize the same dungeon tiles across runs.
- **[[Combat]]:** The GLOO Cannon's dual-purpose design (tool + weapon) is the template for Wayfinder's combat-as-one-verb ethos — items used in gathering or traversal should create emergent combat options without being labeled "combat items."
