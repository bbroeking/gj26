# Pathfinder Wrath of the Righteous

Sprawling isometric CRPG (2021, Owlcat Games) adapting the Pathfinder 2018 "Wrath of the Righteous" adventure path — notable for its two-layer progression (party class + mythic path) that lets players become genuinely demi-divine, and a strategic Crusade layer borrowed from Heroes of Might and Magic.

## Design

- **Mythic paths as identity transformation.** Beyond the standard Pathfinder class/archetype ladder, players unlock a Mythic Path at the story's midpoint — Angel, Demon, Lich, Azata, Aeon, Trickster, Gold Dragon, or Swarm-That-Walks. Each path is not just a power bundle: it rewrites how the character is perceived, unlocks path-exclusive dialogue, and changes the story's ending. The Lich player commands undead armies and can turn companions into thralls; the Trickster subverts every serious moment with absurdist comedy. The mythic system is the game's most cited design innovation.
- **Class/archetype depth from tabletop.** WotR implements the full Pathfinder 1e ruleset with over 25 classes and hundreds of archetypes. Multi-classing and archetype stacking produce combinatorially vast build space — a deliberate contrast to streamlined modern CRPGs. The design philosophy holds that complexity is a feature for the target audience.
- **Crusade as a meta-layer.** Players command the Fifth Crusade against the demon-overrun Worldwound as both field general and party leader. The Crusade system is a turn-based strategy layer (explicitly inspired by Heroes of Might and Magic) where the player manages armies, builds fortifications, and fights large-scale tactical battles. Some players find it disruptive; others cite it as making the "you're in charge of a war" framing legible. It can be toggled to automated mode for players who want to ignore it.
- **Choice and consequence at scale.** The game tracks companion approval, faction alignments, and mythic path divergence across 100+ hours. Multiple endings change substantially based on mythic path, major story decisions, and companion outcomes. Some endings require behaving consistently with a chosen mythic identity across the full game — not just a final dialogue pick.
- **Companions with full reactivity.** All 11 companions have personal quest arcs, disapproval/approval mechanics, and path-specific dialogue. Some companions leave or become hostile based on mythic path choice (a Demon-path player cannot retain a devout angel-companion without specific dialogue management).

## Implementation

- **Unity engine, Owlcat's second Pathfinder title.** Owlcat had shipped Kingmaker in 2018, a critically uneven release plagued by bugs and unfun kingdom-management. WotR addressed both: smoother kingdom (Crusade) design, better optimization, and a patch cadence that fixed bugs within months rather than years. Metacritic 83/100 PC, sold 1 million copies by January 2023.
- **Kickstarter-funded, $2 million goal.** The campaign confirmed player demand for high-complexity tabletop-faithful CRPGs — a market signal that Larian's Divinity success (2014/2017) had expanded.

## Why it matters

WotR is the high-water mark for **mythic progression as narrative identity**: a second progression axis where power and story meaning are fully entangled. The design proves that a second "tier" of advancement (on top of standard leveling) can be the game's most memorable system if it has genuine narrative weight rather than just stat scaling. It also demonstrates that a strategy meta-layer can coexist with an isometric RPG — at the cost of focus.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Trades and Leveling]]:** The mythic path system models a Wayfinder possibility: a "master's mark" tier unlocked deep into a Trade that changes how the world sees the player-character, not just their numbers. The insight — advancement beyond a level cap should feel like a qualitative identity shift, not more of the same.
- **[[Skills]]:** WotR's archetype stacking (dozens of micro-choices compounding over 20 levels) is the complexity extreme Wayfinder should NOT replicate for a cozy game — but the design principle that each sub-choice should feel meaningfully different is transferable to hotbar Skill selection.
- **[[Combat]]:** The Crusade layer's tonal friction (suddenly playing a wargame) is a cautionary note for Wayfinder: any meta-system (chart management, town economy) that hijacks the game's feel risks alienating players who came for the core loop.
