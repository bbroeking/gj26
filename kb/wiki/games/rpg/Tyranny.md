# Tyranny

Isometric CRPG (2016, Obsidian Entertainment / Paradox Interactive) — a tactical RPG set in a world where evil has already won, casting the player as a Fatebinder serving the overlord Kyros, with a modular spell-crafting system, dual Favor/Wrath faction reputation, and a Conquest prologue that pre-writes the world state before the game begins.

## Design

- **Conquest prologue — the world shaped before play begins.** Before the main game loads, players narrate how Kyros's armies conquered the Tiers across a series of map-based decisions (how was each region pacified? which atrocities were committed? which factions were favored?). These choices alter NPC attitudes, available companions, loot in the world, and dialogue options throughout the entire run — backstory is a character-creation mechanic.
- **Dual faction reputation: Favor and Wrath.** Players simultaneously accumulate Favor (loyalty/approval) and Wrath (fear/defiance) with each faction and companion. Both tracks unlock unique dialogue, abilities, and quests — high Wrath with the Scarlet Chorus unlocks intimidation options unavailable at high Favor, and vice versa. The design rejects the assumption that reputation is a single approval axis and rewards committing to a relationship style.
- **Modular spell-crafting.** Players combine elemental cores (fire, frost, electricity, void, arcane) with expression accents (projectile, AoE, aura, self) and effect accents (damage type, status, duration). Modifications cost Lore points, creating meaningful trade-offs between power and versatility. Unlike most CRPGs where spells are pre-defined, Tyranny's spells are literally constructed by the player and feel personally authored.
- **Edict system.** Kyros wields magical superweapons — continent-affecting Edicts that destroy armies or curse regions. The player eventually discovers how to cast Edicts independently, fundamentally destabilizing the power structure. Edicts function as both lore worldbuilding and a late-game mechanical revelation.
- **Classless skill progression through use.** Skills improve by using them, and characters can freely hybridize (a warrior who casts can develop both trees). Director Brian Heins designed this to allow mid-game pivots without penalty — players who discover they dislike their build aren't locked in.

## Implementation

- **Modified Unity** (Obsidian's Pillars of Eternity codebase), enabling rapid development from an established CRPG foundation. Released November 10, 2016 on Windows, macOS, Linux.
- Metacritic 80/100 (67 reviews). Critics praised world-building and moral complexity; the most-cited weakness was an abrupt narrative ending (the game's short length relative to its ambition — Obsidian reportedly had more story planned that was cut).
- The game's central conceit — playing an agent of an evil empire without a redemption arc being the "correct" choice — was commercially risky and did not match Baldur's Gate 3-scale expectations. Its modest commercial footprint belies its design influence, particularly on how faction reputation systems are now discussed in design postmortems.

## Why it matters

Tyranny's most transferable insight is the **Conquest prologue**: by letting players author their backstory through a pre-game decision sequence, it makes every run feel personally owned from the first scene rather than treating character creation as a stats screen. The dual Favor/Wrath system is the cleanest implementation of "reputation as multi-dimensional relationship" in the CRPG genre — it shows that approval-tracking can reward commitment to a *style* rather than optimization of a single dial. The modular spell system is the benchmark for player-authored magic in RPGs.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Charts]] / [[Affixes]]:** The Conquest prologue is a direct model for how Wayfinder's chart-inscription loop could work as pre-run authoring — players don't just pick affixes from a menu; they narrate (or inscribe) what kind of dungeon they're going into, with downstream consequences in room composition and enemy behavior.
- **[[Skills]]:** Tyranny's modular spell-crafting is the design ideal for Wayfinder's Ink system or any future skill-combination mechanic — combining core elements with expression modifiers to create personally authored abilities rather than selecting from a fixed list.
- **[[Balance Philosophy]]:** The dual Favor/Wrath model is relevant to any Wayfinder mechanic that tracks player reputation with dungeon factions or NPC guilds — allowing both aggressive and cooperative playstyles to generate distinct (not just better/worse) outcomes.
