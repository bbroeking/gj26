# Heroes of the Storm

5v5 MOBA (2015, Blizzard Entertainment) that stripped individual-carry mechanics in favour of team-wide systems, multi-battleground variety, and Blizzard IP crossover — then entered maintenance mode after its esports program was cancelled in December 2018.

## Design

- **Core departure from LoL/Dota:** No individual gold income; the entire team shares a single XP pool that levels all heroes simultaneously. This collapses the "farming vs. fighting" tension that defines the genre and forces constant team cooperation. A solo player cannot out-farm their way to victory.
- **Multiple battlegrounds:** 15 themed maps (Haunted Mines, Garden of Terror, Battlefield of Eternity, etc.), each with a unique secondary objective that grants a powerful macro effect — skeleton army, garden terror plant, angel/demon boss. Match variety is built into map rotation rather than a single canonical arena.
- **Talent trees:** Heroes unlock talents at fixed level thresholds, choosing from several options that modify or augment existing abilities. Level 10 offers a choice between two Heroic (ultimate) abilities; level 20 upgrades the chosen Heroic. The system is shallower than Dota 2's item builds but more accessible than bare-ability heroes.
- **Hero roster:** 89 heroes drawn from Warcraft, Diablo, StarCraft, and Overwatch universes. Roles: tank, bruiser, ranged assassin, melee assassin, healer, support.
- **Esports:** Heroes Global Championship (HGC) and Heroes of the Dorm ran for several seasons. On December 13, 2018, Blizzard cancelled both programs, citing a shift of developers to other projects — effectively defunding the esports scene overnight with immediate team disbandments.
- **Maintenance mode:** Major development ended July 2022. The game still receives seasonal balance patches and event content post-Microsoft acquisition, but no new heroes or battlegrounds.

## Implementation

- **Engine:** Built on the StarCraft II Galaxy engine (shared codebase with SC2: Legacy of the Void). The engine was originally designed around the Galaxy Map Editor; Heroes grew from internal prototypes made in that editor.
- **Netcode:** Not separately documented; assumed to follow Blizzard's client-server authoritative model used across their portfolio (Battle.net infrastructure).
- **Platform:** PC (Windows/macOS) via Battle.net launcher.

## Why it matters

- **Team-design experiment:** HotS proved the genre could survive without individual gold and last-hitting, but also that the audience for this design was smaller than hoped — the title never reached LoL or Dota 2 peak concurrents despite Blizzard's IP and marketing resources.
- **Esports cancellation lesson:** Rapid shutdown of the HGC without transition plans caused significant goodwill damage. Players and pros pointed to the abrupt cancellation (mid-season contracts, no wind-down period) as a model of how *not* to sunset competitive support. Compare to Paragon's full game shutdown for a spectrum of managed vs. unmanaged exits.
- **IP crossover ceiling:** Despite iconic characters (Diablo, Kerrigan, Tracer), cross-IP novelty did not sustain long-term engagement. Differentiation in MOBA must come from mechanics, not roster recognizability.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- [[Multiplayer Co-op]]: Shared XP and the elimination of individual carry mechanics map directly onto Wayfinder's co-op dungeon philosophy — runs should feel jointly won, not attributable to one player's farming lead. Consider shared resource pools for co-op chart runs.
- [[Combat]]: Multi-battleground secondary objectives (each with a distinct mechanic) are an inspiring template for Wayfinder's dungeon biome variety — each crypt variant could have a single standout secondary mechanic rather than a uniform encounter list.
- [[Balance Philosophy]]: The HGC cancellation shows the risk of over-investing in organised competitive play before the casual player base is large enough to sustain it — Wayfinder should prove casual retention first.
