# Dominion

Board/card game (Donald X. Vaccarino / Rio Grande Games, 2008) — the genre-founding deck-builder: every card type (resource, action, victory point) lives inside the deck you're building mid-game, creating a feedback loop between acquisition and engine power that all subsequent deckbuilders are measured against.

## Design

- **Everything in the deck**: Vaccarino's core insight was radical: treasure cards, action cards, and victory point cards all go into the same deck. Resources aren't a separate track — they cycle back through draws as income. This eliminated setup complexity and made the deck itself the game's primary artifact.
- **Supply piles**: A shared central tableau of 10 Kingdom card types (chosen from ~25 per base set) plus fixed Treasure and Victory cards. Players spend Treasure to buy from the supply; bought cards go to discard and cycle in. The tableau is the "board" — no map, no board space, just the cards between players.
- **Victory point dilution**: Provinces (6 VP, 8 cost) anchor the end-game. Buying Victory cards dilutes your deck with dead draws, creating a tension between engine power and point acquisition that is the game's central timing puzzle. Vaccarino designed three VP tiers (Estate/Duchy/Province) to allow different game-speed strategies.
- **Draw five, reshuffle**: Players draw five cards per turn, ensuring they see their entire deck roughly every two turns. This short feedback loop is deliberate: players feel their purchases quickly, making deckbuilding satisfying at short timescales.
- **Attack symmetry**: Attack cards affect all opponents equally; targeted attacks that let players form coalitions were explicitly excluded. This keeps the game political-free and makes it scale cleanly from 2–6 players.
- **Expansions**: 16 expansions as of 2024, each introducing new card types without modifying base rules (Duration cards, Debt tokens, Night phase, etc.). The genre-founding mechanic has proven remarkably extensible.
- **No randomness in play**: Once shuffled, the deck plays out deterministically. All RNG is in shuffle order; no dice, no coin flips. This makes skill expression about deck composition and sequencing, not luck of external resolution.

## Implementation

- Physical card game; Dominion Online (Shuffled Ink) is the official digital implementation.
- Won Spiel des Jahres and Deutscher Spiele Preis (2009). Over 2.5 million physical copies sold by 2017.
- The simplicity of the design (no board, minimal components beyond cards) enabled rapid prototyping — Vaccarino developed the game alone before bringing it to a publisher.

## Why it matters

Dominion invented deck-building as a standalone mechanic divorced from collectibility. Every deckbuilder since — Slay the Spire, Inscryption, Cobalt Core, Hearthstone's draft mode — inherits Dominion's core loop: start weak, buy power, thin the deck, time the end-game. The supply-pile tableau is also the cleanest model for "a shared pool of available options that creates meaningful competition without direct conflict."

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Economy]]**: Dominion's VP-dilution timing puzzle is a model for Wayfinder's chart loop: accumulating chart-output (trophies, boss seals) should impose a cost or constraint similar to buying Provinces — at some point collecting the reward dilutes the power to keep collecting.
- **[[Affixes]]**: The Kingdom card selection mechanic (10 of ~25 possible stacks) is a direct precedent for per-run affix pools. Each run constraining the available affix space would force players to adapt their strategy to what's offered rather than defaulting to a fixed build.
- **[[Balance Philosophy]]**: Vaccarino's "attack symmetry" rule — no targeted politics — is a live lesson for Wayfinder's co-op design. PvP-adjacent mechanics (competing for gather nodes, chart leaderboards) should avoid designs that create negative-sum player targeting.
