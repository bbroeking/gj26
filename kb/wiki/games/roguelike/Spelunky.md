# Spelunky

A freeware action platformer-roguelite by Derek Yu, released in 2008 (HD remake 2012, Spelunky 2 in 2020), that fused real-time physics platformer mechanics with roguelike procedural generation and permadeath — defining the "roguelite" sub-genre and establishing the ghost timer as a canonical pacing mechanic.

## Design

- **Hybrid genre:** Spelunky applies roguelike principles (procedural levels, permadeath, no save-reloading) to a real-time physics platformer. The player has precise control over jump arcs and interactions, so death feels earned rather than arbitrary.
- **Shared ruleset:** Every entity in the game obeys the same physics and interaction rules — NPCs, enemies, the player, and projectiles. A boulder traps a shopkeeper; an arrow trap kills the player if they step on the trigger. This consistency is what Yu called the "literacy of the system": players learn the rules once and apply them universally.
- **Level generation — 4×4 grid with room templates:** Each level is a 4×4 grid of 16 rooms. The generator first traces a connected path from the entry room (top) to the exit (bottom), then fills remaining cells with templates chosen based on their position relative to the solution path. Room templates are hand-authored, populated with probabilistic enemy/trap/treasure placement. The result balances authored feel with randomness.
- **The Ghost — pacing timer:** Approximately 2–2.5 minutes after entering a level (3 minutes in Spelunky 2), a spectral Ghost spawns and pursues the player, passes through walls, and kills on contact. It cannot be defeated. This converts exploration time into a hard resource — players who linger to collect every gem or secret must weigh the ghost risk. It is a non-punitive timer: the ghost only matters if you are slow.
- **Shortcut system:** Three shortcuts (to the Jungle, Ice Caves, and Temple) can be unlocked by delivering items to Tunnel Man. These allow players to practice later biomes without replaying the early game, reducing practice friction without removing the challenge of full runs.
- **Permadeath:** A single death resets the entire run. There is no mid-run save. The only persistence across runs is unlocked shortcuts and some item knowledge.
- **Daily Challenge:** A fixed-seed run shared globally, scored against other players — one attempt per day. Established the daily-challenge format later widely copied.

## Implementation

- **Engine:** GameMaker Studio (original 2008 freeware, Windows). The 2012 HD remake targeted Xbox Live Arcade (and later PC/PS3/Vita/iOS/Switch); source code of the original was released in 2009 under a non-commercial license.
- **Procgen approach:** The 4×4 grid is populated by selecting room templates from category-appropriate sets. Template selection is seeded per run; the solution path through the grid guarantees the level is completable. Difficulty-scaling room pools (easy/medium/hard) skew template selection as floors increase.
- **Physics:** Tile-based physics with discrete grid movement for terrain but continuous motion for entities. Inspired by Super Mario Bros. for feel; enemies interact with all the same physics the player does (weight, knock-back, traps).
- **Sequels and influence:** Spelunky 2 (2020, BlitWorks/Mossmouth) refined the generator and added a layered cave structure with alternative routes. Both games support 4-player local co-op.

## Why it matters

Spelunky is the definitive argument that *roguelike principles improve real-time action games.* Its ghost timer is the elegant answer to the food-clock problem: a pressure mechanic with no resource to track, no number to watch — just a visible, terrifying consequence. The shared-ruleset design (every entity obeys the same physics) is the template for "simulation-first" level design where the content is generated by rule interactions, not authored scripting.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Dungeon Generation]]:** The 4×4 grid with solution-path-first template selection is directly applicable to Wayfinder's dungeon layouts — a hand-authored template library guarantees completability while random population provides variety. Worth studying alongside Rogue's 3×3 room grid.
- **[[Chart Loop]]:** Spelunky's ghost-timer is a model for chart pacing pressure in Wayfinder. A chart could introduce a "surge" mechanic — escalating danger after a soft timer — without a visible countdown, preserving tension without UI clutter.
- **[[Affixes]]:** Spelunky's biome-to-biome shifts (Mines → Jungle → Ice Caves → Temple) function like affix tiers: each zone introduces a new threat vocabulary that recombines with the player's existing toolkit. Wayfinder's dungeon affix tiers could layer similarly.
