# Salt and Sanctuary

2D action-RPG (2016, Ska Studios) — a two-person indie soulslike that translates Dark Souls' bonfire/death/progression systems into a hand-drawn 2D Metroidvania, adding a sanctuary-building mechanic, an expansive class-agnostic skill tree, and local co-op.

## Design

- **Direct 3D-to-2D translation of soulslike grammar.** Salt replaces Souls as the unified XP/currency dropped on death (retrieved at the bloodstain equivalent). Sanctuaries replace bonfires — rest points that heal and respawn enemies. The design philosophy stated by Ska Studios co-founder Michelle Juett Silva: "Dark Souls is a 3D version of Castlevania: Symphony of the Night." The game validates that thesis by reversing the vector.
- **Sanctuary system.** Players dedicate sanctuaries to one of several religious creeds. Each creed unlocks different merchants, blacksmiths, and upgrade vendors at that location. Choosing incompatible creeds bars access to certain vendors — an alignment layer that creates gentle per-run specialization pressure without full class locking.
- **Class-agnostic skill tree.** Hundreds of skill-tree nodes cover every weapon type, magic school, and passive stat. Any character can theoretically invest in any branch, but scaling costs make multi-class characters inefficient. This mirrors Dark Souls' open stat allocation but presented as a visible graph players navigate rather than a hidden number.
- **2D combat breadth.** The game features multiple weapon categories — each with distinct 2D move-sets (overhead swings, thrusts, whips, spells). The jump and ledge-grab traverse system adds vertical combat options not available in 3D soulslikes, creating attack angles and enemy positioning unique to the 2D space.
- **Local co-op throughout.** The entire game supports two-player local co-op, including boss fights. This was a deliberate commitment made at the start of development by the two-person team — unusual for the genre and the studio size.
- **Asynchronous messages.** Players can leave and read messages from other players in-world, mirroring Dark Souls' soapstone system adapted to 2D.

## Implementation

- Engine: Ska Studios proprietary (same technology as Charlie Murder and the Dishwasher series).
- Developer: Ska Studios — a two-person studio (James Silva, art/programming; Michelle Juett Silva, writing/design).
- Metacritic: 84/100 (PS4, PC), 82/100 (Switch), 80/100 (Xbox One).
- Release: March 15, 2016 (PS4); ported to PC, PS Vita, Switch, Xbox One over 2016–2019.
- 600+ items across weapon categories, armor sets, and consumables.
- No publicly stated sales figures; commercial success sufficient to fund the sequel Salt and Sacrifice (2022).

## Why it matters

Salt and Sanctuary demonstrates that soulslike systems are genre-agnostic and scale down to a two-person studio. The sanctuary/creed system is one of the neatest multiplications of the bonfire concept: resting is necessary, but whom you rest with creates faction-based resource availability. It also establishes that 2D and soulslike are not mutually exclusive — the genre's soul is in resource risk, checkpoint sparsity, and deliberate enemy patterning, all of which survive the dimension reduction. For small-team developers, the game proves that scope control (two weapons categories per difficulty layer, not dozens) is compatible with systemic depth.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Sanctuary as faction hub.** Wayfinder's town (Bramblewood) could borrow the creed-per-sanctuary pattern: which NPCs are available at a rest node might vary by chart affixes or run state, creating a soft "allegiance" feel without locking players out of core content. See [[Multiplayer Co-op]], [[Bosses]].
- **2D combat as reference for attack-angle breadth.** Salt and Sanctuary's jump-attack and overhead-swing variety maps to a design question Wayfinder will face: does the one-verb combat model allow enough attack angles to feel expressive across different enemy types? See [[Combat]], [[Camera and Game Feel]].
- **Small-team scope model.** A two-person team shipped a genre-defining entry by limiting total content and leveraging systemic depth. Relevant to Wayfinder's current sprint-sliced roadmap. See [[Design Influences]].
