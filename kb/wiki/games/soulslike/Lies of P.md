# Lies of P

Action-RPG (2023, Round8 Studio/Neowiz) — a soulslike retelling of Pinocchio set in Belle Époque-era Krat, distinguished by a mandatory perfect-block parry system, modular weapon assembly, and a binary lie/truth morality layer that alters the narrative ending.

## Design

- **Perfect Guard (parry-first design).** Rather than dodge-rolling as the primary evasion tool, Lies of P centers combat on the "Perfect Guard" — blocking at the exact moment of impact. Repeated perfect blocks build stagger on enemies, opening them to fatal attacks. Blocking without perfect timing still deals "chip damage" to P's health, but a successful counterattack restores the lost health (similar to Bloodborne's regain system). This creates a push-pull loop: take chip damage → aggress to recover → block again.
- **Weapon assembly.** Every weapon splits into a blade and a handle, each independently upgradeable and combinable. Swapping handles grants different move-sets and Fable Arts (weapon skills activated by a separate Fable charge). Players discussing "best combinations" rather than "best weapons" was the designer's explicit goal — the system drives community theorycrafting rather than meta convergence.
- **P-Organ progression.** Bosses drop Quartz used to unlock nodes in the P-Organ skill tree, providing passive upgrades (extra heals, guard counters, etc.). This separates boss rewards from standard level-up progression and gives boss kills a second layer of satisfaction beyond story advancement.
- **Lies mechanic.** At certain NPC dialogue prompts, P can tell the truth or lie. Choices accumulate to influence the ending and affect certain NPCs' fates. Mechanically minor, but thematically central: lying is coded as humanization — a puppet learning to be deceptive. The system avoids morality meters, delivering consequence through story payoff rather than stat changes.
- **Boss design quality.** The base game's boss roster received strong praise; the DLC expansion Overture (released 2025–26) elevated the bar further, with critics citing its bosses among the strongest in the soulslike genre.
- **No multiplayer.** Lies of P is strictly single-player — a deliberate choice that allowed the team (100 developers) to focus on balance tuning around a solo experience.

## Implementation

- Engine: Unreal Engine 4.
- Developer: Round8 Studio (subsidiary of Neowiz, previously known for multiplayer titles like Bless Unleashed — Lies of P was a deliberate pivot to single-player AAA).
- Metacritic: 80/100 (PS5), 83/100 (PC), 85/100 (Xbox Series X/S).
- Sales: 3 million units by June 2025; 4 million by March 2026.
- Awards: Apple Design Award 2024 (Visuals and Graphics), App Store 2023 Mac Game of the Year.
- DLC: Lies of P: Overture — released 2025, expanded the game's boss count and lore.

## Why it matters

Lies of P proves that soulslike is now a viable genre for non-Japanese studios working at AAA production values, and that the parry-first design space (pioneered by [[Sekiro Shadows Die Twice]]) can be meaningfully iterated on. The weapon assembly system is the clearest implementation of "combinatorial item design drives community" yet shipped in the genre: two systems (blades + handles) multiplied together generate thousands of combinations, far more than a single-tier item list. The chip-damage-then-regain loop also demonstrates that both Bloodborne-style aggression and Sekiro-style precision can coexist in a single combat system without forcing players to choose one.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Parry/regain as aggression incentive.** The chip-then-regain loop pressures players to stay aggressive after taking damage, which aligns well with Wayfinder's combat-as-one-verb philosophy — death should feel like a failure to press advantage, not a failure to hide. See [[Combat]].
- **Weapon assembly = combinatorial gear.** The blade-handle split maps to Wayfinder's potential for modular gear with affix combinations — swapping a handle changes the Fable Art just as swapping an affix changes a run's feel. See [[Items and Gear]], [[Affixes]].
- **Boss-drops-unlock-progression.** The Quartz / P-Organ system — where boss kills fund a skill tree separate from level-up — is transferable to Wayfinder's trophy chain logic: boss trophies unlock deeper dens, but could also directly fund a permanent upgrade tree. See [[Bosses]].
