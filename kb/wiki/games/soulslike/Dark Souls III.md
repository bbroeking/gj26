# Dark Souls III

Action-RPG (2016, FromSoftware/Bandai Namco) — the capstone of the original trilogy, refining Bloodborne's speed and aggression into a stamina-gated framework with weapon arts, a split Estus system, and some of the most diverse boss encounters in the soulslike canon.

## Design

- **Speed shift from DS1/DS2.** Combat is notably faster than its predecessors, influenced directly by Bloodborne: dodge rolls have standardized i-frames (the problematic Adaptation stat was removed), enemies chain 5–7 hit combos, and counter-attacking windows narrow substantially. This rewards aggressive reads over passive blocking.
- **Split Estus Flask.** Health and FP (Focus Points) share a single flask economy — players allocate charges between HP and FP before each area. This makes spell/skill builds a genuine resource-management choice rather than a passive bonus.
- **Weapon Arts (Skills).** Every weapon carries a unique skill activated with FP, from parry to spinning attacks to magical projectiles. This system presages Elden Ring's ash-of-war layer and substantially expands build expression beyond stat allocation.
- **Boss design breadth.** Encounters range from gimmick fights (Deacons of the Deep — crowd management) to pure-reaction tests (Pontiff Sulyvahn — aggressive twin-sword combos). The DLC bosses (Sister Friede, Slave Knight Gael) are widely considered the peak of the series' boss craft, with multi-phase transformations that recontextualize the fight mid-encounter.
- **Linear world geography.** Unlike the seamless hub of Lordran, DS3's world is mostly forward-branching — areas rarely loop back and interconnect. This is the most-criticized structural regression from [[Dark Souls]].
- **Poise controversy.** The poise system exists in the data but was shipped effectively disabled, making slow heavy weapons non-viable and funneling players toward fast weapons. An enduring design debate about intentionality vs. accident.
- **Co-op phantom scaling flaw.** Summoning a co-op phantom does not meaningfully scale boss health or aggression, making encounters trivial: the boss fixates on one phantom while the second attacks freely from behind.

## Implementation

- Engine: FromSoftware proprietary (SoulsEngine lineage).
- Metacritic: 89/100 (PC, PS4), 87/100 (Xbox One). IGN 9.5/10; The Guardian 5/5; Polygon 7/10.
- Shipped 3 million copies within two months of release (April 2016); exceeded 10 million by May 2020 — Bandai Namco's fastest-selling title until [[Elden Ring]].
- DLC: Ashes of Ariandel (Oct 2016) and The Ringed City (Mar 2017); the latter's Gael boss fight is frequently cited as the emotional endpoint of the entire Dark Souls saga.
- Director: Hidetaka Miyazaki (also directing DS1, Bloodborne, Sekiro, Elden Ring).

## Why it matters

DS3 demonstrates that a soulslike sequel can tighten and refine mechanics without reinventing structure — and reveals the cost when it doesn't. The split Estus economy is one of the cleanest resource-dilemma designs in the genre: every flask charge allocated to FP is a HP charge sacrificed. The weapon arts system proved that soulslike melee can carry build identity beyond stats. Conversely, the disabled poise system and passive co-op scaling are cautionary notes: shipped mechanical imbalances compound rapidly in a genre where encounter fairness is the core contract with the player.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **Split resource economy.** The Estus HP/FP split maps directly to any Wayfinder consumable that serves dual purposes (e.g., a restore item that could be used offensively). The allocation-before-delving pattern is transferable to chart preparation. See [[Combat]], [[Items and Gear]].
- **Weapon arts as identity layer.** DS3's per-weapon skills foreshadow the Wayfinder [[Skills]] hotbar: each equipped item implies a playstyle, not just a stat boost. This is the "combat-as-one-verb" design goal concretized.
- **Co-op phantom scaling.** DS3's failure here is a direct lesson for Wayfinder's 2–4 player mode — boss HP, aggression, and phase triggers must scale per-player-count or the encounter evaporates. See [[Multiplayer Co-op]], [[Bosses]].
