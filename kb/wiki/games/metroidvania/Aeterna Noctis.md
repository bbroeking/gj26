# Aeterna Noctis

2D Metroidvania-Soulslike hybrid (December 2021, Aeternum Game Studios, Spain), playing as the King of Darkness in a 16-zone interconnected world, distinguished by precision platforming challenge modes, Souls-derived death mechanics, and a throne-bonfire rest system.

## Design

- **Metroidvania + Soulslike structure.** 16 interconnected zones in classic non-linear map layout. Thrones serve as bonfire equivalents — resting respawns surrounding enemies (but not the throne's immediate zone). Death drops items; one attempt to reclaim them before permanent loss mirrors Dark Souls' bloodstain loop.
- **Dual difficulty modes.** Standard mode emphasises action challenge; "Noctis Mode" adds hardcore precision-platforming difficulty on top of combat, explicitly targeting players who want both Soulslike attrition and Celeste-tier platforming in one package.
- **Skill tree + regained abilities.** The King of Darkness recovers movement and combat abilities progressively (~20-hour campaign). A first-tier skill tree customises combat stats (damage, critical chance), though reviews note this layer lacks depth vs. genre peers.
- **Fast travel via B-69** — a robot operating a space-warping train between select zones, reducing backtrack friction without undermining exploration tension.
- **Movement exploitation by design.** Level geometry is built to allow players to find multiple routes through skill expression, rewarding mastery of the movement set rather than gating strictly to single-path solutions.

## Implementation

- Engine: **Unity** (confirmed via PCGamingWiki and developer metadata).
- Developer: Aeternum Game Studios, headquartered in Talavera de la Reina, Spain — a small Spanish indie team releasing on PS5, Xbox Series X/S, Nintendo Switch, and PC simultaneously at launch.
- Metacritic scores ranged 67–72 across platforms; OpenCritic showed 50% recommendation rate. Critical consensus: competent execution of both genre pillars, but combat responsiveness and platforming hitbox precision were the main friction points ("axe enemies attack faster than the player sword swings").
- A free sequel, *Aeterna Noctis: Caos*, was announced post-launch, suggesting the studio planned to iterate the IP.

## Why it matters

- One of the cleanest live examples of **Metroidvania + Soulslike genre fusion**: the bonfire/death loop and map structure coexist without either genre pillar undermining the other — useful as a reference for what works and what stumbles.
- Dual-mode difficulty (action vs. precision platforming) is a design pattern for studios that want to serve two distinct player appetites with one codebase rather than separate game modes.
- The B-69 fast-travel system (narrative-flavored transport vs. teleport menu) shows how to reduce backtrack cost while preserving world coherence.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Dungeon Generation]]** — Aeterna Noctis's 16-zone non-linear map with enemy-respawn-on-rest is a reference for how Wayfinder chart runs could handle enemy density resets between dungeon attempts; the bonfire → throne rename also shows how theming the rest point matters for world feel.
- **[[Combat]]** — The Soulslike death-drop mechanic is explicitly *not* cozy; Wayfinder's single-verb combat should learn from where Aeterna Noctis's combat sluggishness (timing mismatch) created frustration rather than mastery satisfaction.
- **[[Skills]]** — Shallow first-tier skill tree critique is a cautionary note: if Wayfinder's Trade leveling provides only numeric stat bumps at early tiers, player engagement drops. Unlock-new-verb > increase-old-stat at every level.
