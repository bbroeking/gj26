# Axiom Verge

Sci-fi Metroidvania (2015, Thomas Happ Games, PS4/PC) developed entirely by one person — developer, artist, and composer — over five years, distinguished by a "glitch gun" that corrupts enemy and environment behavior as a traversal and combat mechanic.

## Design

- **Address Disruptor (glitch gun).** Fires waves of blue corruption rectangles that alter enemy behavior (making them passive, erratic, or easier to kill) and blocks (making solid tiles passable, or vice versa). The mechanic is named after memory-address hacking and draws on the designer's childhood memories of Game Genie codes and cartridge-swap glitches. It extends traditional Metroidvania ability-gating into the fabric of the environment itself — not "use key to open door" but "corrupt reality to pass through."
- **Passcode Tool.** Inspired by NES Metroid's password system; in-world strings of characters unlock items and secrets, encouraging experimentation and community decryption. Creates a meta-layer of exploration outside the game's spatial map.
- **Glitch World.** A hidden alternate layer of the map accessible via specific corruption moves, containing lore fragments and meta-commentary. Parallels the Guacamelee "dark world" but framed as actual system instability.
- **Drone.** A deployable unit that can enter spaces too small for the protagonist Trace, trigger remote switches, and navigate alternate paths — a second traversal agent that expands the possibility space without adding movement abilities to the player character.
- **60+ weapons and power-ups.** Deliberately wide arsenal to support varied playstyle expression; each weapon also functions differently against specific enemy types, making the glitch system layer on top of a deep combat system.
- **Art-first development.** Happ sketched every environmental tileset and room layout on paper for months before writing a line of code, producing a cohesive visual and spatial logic before implementation.

## Implementation

- Thomas Happ worked solo from March 2010 to March 2015 — five years — while employed full-time at Petroglyph Games. He served as programmer, pixel artist, and composer throughout.
- Engine: Microsoft XNA (Windows) / MonoGame (ports). PS4 launch March 31 2015 (NA); PC May 14 2015; later Vita, Wii U, Switch, Xbox One.
- Metacritic: 84/100 (PS4), 89/100 (Wii U). Giant Bomb: 5/5.
- Sequel Axiom Verge 2 (August 2021) also solo-developed by Happ — a rare case of a one-person studio producing a full franchise.

## Why it matters

Axiom Verge proved that a single person could build a commercially successful, critically credible Metroidvania in the era of AAA incumbents — and that the glitch-as-mechanic concept (corrupting the game's own rules as a power) is a coherent design space, not a novelty. It inspired a wave of one- or two-person Metroidvanias in the late 2010s. The art-first development method is replicable and particularly useful for solo or tiny-team projects where scope control is existential.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Skills]]:** The "corrupt the environment" model suggests a Wayfinder Trade ability that alters a dungeon room's properties (opens a hidden passage, changes a hazard into a resource) — ability as environmental editor rather than mere key.
- **[[Dungeon Generation]]:** The Glitch World (hidden layer under normal geometry) maps directly to crypt dungeon "cursed" variants — same room, alternate tile logic, different spawn table — accessible only by players with the relevant Trade ability.
- **[[Camera and Game Feel]]:** Happ's paper-first tileset design process (rooms sketched before coded) is applicable to Wayfinder's dungeon room authoring: design the visual grammar of each room type before parameterizing it for generation.
