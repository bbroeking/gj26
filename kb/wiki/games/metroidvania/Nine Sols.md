# Nine Sols

Hand-drawn 2D Metroidvania (2024, Red Candle Games, PC/Switch/PS4-5/Xbox) that grafts Sekiro-style deflection combat onto a Hollow Knight-scaled exploration game, set in a "Taopunk" world fusing Taoism with cyberpunk.

## Design

- **Deflect-first combat loop.** Unlike dodge-centric action games, Nine Sols makes parrying the primary defensive verb. A correctly timed deflect absorbs a Qi charge; stacked Qi is loaded into explosive talismans placed on enemies; talismans are then detonated for burst damage. The loop — bait attack → deflect → tag → detonate — must be internalized for every encounter, and each of the nine Sol bosses requires learning their specific deflect windows.
- **Talisman as charged attack bridge.** Rather than mashing through a combo, players must earn their burst damage by surviving enough deflects to charge up. This paces fights into readable rhythmic exchanges, not frantic button-mashing.
- **Nine boss rulers as the pacing skeleton.** The nine Sols are both narrative antagonists and mechanical hurdles — each demands mastery of the deflect vocabulary the player has built so far. They function like [[Super Metroid]]'s boss gates but with Sekiro's read-and-respond precision demand.
- **Metroidvania exploration layer.** Between bosses, New Kunlun is a contiguous interconnected world with wall-run, grapple, and ability gates. The exploration tone echoes [[Hollow Knight]] — atmospheric, melancholy, reveals lore through found items and environmental staging rather than cutscenes.
- **Taopunk aesthetic as coherent identity.** The game's world (New Kunlun), protagonist (Yi, reimagined as a Taoist archer Hou Yi in sci-fi), and soundtrack (Taiwanese Hokkien indie rock) are designed as a unified cultural statement, not just a visual skin. This coherence makes the world feel authored rather than assembled.

## Implementation

- Red Candle Games (Taiwan), third title after Detention (2017) and Devotion (2019). Team: 6 founders grown to 10+ full-time over ~8 years as a studio. Nine Sols development: ~5 years.
- Engine: Unity. Full hand-drawn 2D animation pipeline — no procedural bone rigs; each frame authored.
- PC (Windows/macOS): May 29 2024. Console: November 26 2024.
- Metacritic: 84/100. Described by Rock Paper Shotgun as converting platformer skeptics. Steam reception: Overwhelmingly Positive.

## Why it matters

Nine Sols is the clearest proof that Sekiro-style deflection can be made to work in 2D without reducing to a rhythm game. The talisman system adds a strategic charge-and-spend layer that prevents deflection from collapsing into reflex-testing alone. It also demonstrates that a small Taiwanese studio with a clear cultural identity (Taopunk, Hokkien vocals) can build a globally resonant game without genericizing its aesthetic — authenticity is a competitive advantage, not a niche risk.

## Relevance to Wayfinder

- **[[Combat]]:** The deflect → charge → burst loop is a template for Wayfinder's one-verb combat feeling weighty and legible without a complex combo tree. A single parry mechanic with a visible charge meter gives fights a natural push-pull rhythm.
- **[[Bosses]]:** The nine Sols as milestone bosses (each requiring mastery of the current deflect vocabulary) maps to Wayfinder's trophy chain — boss progression that is also skill-ladder progression, not just a stat check.
- **[[Sekiro Shadows Die Twice]]:** Nine Sols is the 2D implementation of Sekiro's core thesis; cross-reference for the theoretical grounding of deflect-based design.
- **[[Camera and Game Feel]]:** Hand-drawn frame-by-frame animation conveys weight and impact at far lower polygon cost than 3D. Red Candle's pipeline is worth studying for Wayfinder's art direction given its chibi-characters-in-3D approach — contrast in what "feel" each method costs.
