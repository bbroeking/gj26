# World Lore

The deeper mythic history of [[Bramblewood]] — the founding, the Bramble Bargain, the Hedgemother, the quiet factions, and the calendar — told through the village's own folk memory rather than omniscient exposition.

> Anti-patterns still apply: no multi-page mythology, no save-the-world stakes, no grim flips. Everything here is suggestion, not pronouncement — a curious player's reward, never a gameplay gate (`docs/WORLD_LORE.md`).

## Founding

The first families came up from Sallow's End three or four winters after the tide receded: hedge-cutters, a wool-comber, and one widowed cooper. They cleared a patch, built nine cottages, dug Old Mother Well, and began *tending* the hedge rather than fighting it. The trimming continues every spring.

> *"We don't tame the brambles. We tend them. There's a difference."* — Maud Pennycress

## The Bramble Bargain

A long-ago Onywyn — the same name as today's Mother Onywyn, no coincidence — bound herself into the bramble during the Hard Frost of the Long Winter to keep the village from freezing. The folk version: she bargained with the hedge — *my thinking, slower than yours, in exchange for one winter.* The winter was bought; the rest is what we live in.

The current Mother Onywyn is Onywyn-the-elder's great-granddaughter. She knows this. She does not speak of it.

## The Hedgemother (boss identity)

The Hedgemother is not a monster. She is Onywyn-the-elder's bound self — not malevolent, not asleep, but *thinking very slowly*, and when she thinks too hard the bramble wakes. Defeating her is not killing her: it is *quieting her thinking* for a season, which is enough.

> *"Some kindnesses are too big to carry. So we plant them, and let the kindness be the place."* — Mother Onywyn

The Hedgemother boss fight is the player approaching that buried kindness — a piece of sacrificed human cognition still running inside a living hedge. The game does not moralize the kill; the boss entry may not happen at all in a light playthrough.

## The Two Soldiers (faction undercurrent)

**Hod Tenter** and **Sir Withering of Trelliswick** are both veterans. Their wars were not the same war.

- Withering fought a *named* war — under banners, with knights, for a duke whose name still appears in old ledgers. He talks about it the way you talk about a school you didn't enjoy.
- Hod fought a *forgotten* war — small, ugly, between hedgerows. He was probably on the wrong side. He has not used a weapon in twenty years, only hammers.

They drink together at Old Mother Well every Pieday and do not speak. The village finds this comforting.

## The Two Herb-Tenders (faction undercurrent)

**Brother Pell** keeps the *Marked Pages* — a cloister index two centuries old, listing which herbs are safe, which are reagent-only, and which are *not for use*. **Mother Onywyn** ignores the index: she uses foxglove, night-bloom, and wishrose without consulting it. She has not yet harmed anyone.

They are unfailingly polite. Cricket carries weekly letters between them. The letters are mostly about weather and Maud's pies. They have never written about the herbs.

> *"Brother Pell would prefer I sleep at night. I prefer Brother Pell prefer that."* — Mother Onywyn

## NPC Web (gossip lines — codex flavor)

- Eldra had tea with Maud every Threnday for thirty-one years.
- Cricket (16, orphan, lives above the cooperage) was taught to read by Sir Withering after the previous letter-carrier died.
- Hod's late wife Bramwen was Quill's aunt. Quill was raised partly at the smithy; Hod is gentler with Quill than anyone and pretends not to be.
- The Cooper sisters (Marra and Wisp) are first cousins of Maud's late husband. Maud deposits only coppers in protest of the bank fee.
- Brother Pell gave Eldra and Cricket each their own psalter for their birthdays. Mother Onywyn was not invited to either occasion at the cloister.

## The Calendar (4 festivals)

| Festival | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **The Quickening** | First week of First-Spring | Imps grow bold; Pell rings the cloister bell at sundown, seven nights running |
| **Lampwright's Eve** | Three nights mid-Last-Spring | Eldra lights lanterns village-wide at dusk; the dark between lights is deliberate; children stay home |
| **Pieday** | Last Sevenday of High-Summer | Maud's pie contest at Old Mother Well; Hod judges; Withering brings a cake and is gently disqualified every year |
| **Last Light** | Winter solstice | The village gathers at the Well after dusk; each family lights a candle; no one speaks until they burn down |

The calendar does not lock to real-world dates. "First-Spring / Last-Spring / High-Summer" are narrative labels; the in-code day cycle (10 min/game-day) runs mechanically.

## Whispered Legends (discovery hooks)

These are folk tales. None are confirmed; some are clearly false. Use in codex landmark entries.

- **The Man at the Bottom of Old Mother Well.** Children say there's a man asleep down there, breathing. Adults laugh. (There isn't. The village laughs *because* there is.)
- **The Seventh Cottage.** Old Wagon Road shows seven cottages from one angle. Walk it and count: always six. The seventh is for whoever was supposed to come and didn't.
- **The Bramble Bargain.** Covered above. Mother Onywyn neither confirms nor denies.
- **Sallow's Tide.** The shipwreck at Sallow's End was killed by a tide that never happened — the tide-tables for that night are clean. The wreck sits at an angle no real wave makes. Nobody has been down to it in fifty years.
- **Linnet's Vow.** Sir Withering's falcon is named Linnet. There used to be *another* Linnet — a person, not a bird. Withering says the bird's name first. The village is kind enough not to ask.

## Places Named But Not Visited

- **Trelliswick** — Withering's homeland, NE, four-day ride. He won't talk about it.
- **Sallow's End** — tidal flat SW, three hours' walk. The shipwreck; the cousins.
- **Hag's Furrow** — wild hedge-line E; Onywyn's foraging ground; Cricket won't deliver there.
- **The Marl** — marshy basin at the far end of Cricket's letter route. Three days; he hates it; he goes anyway.
- **Coopers' Hold** *(place)* — the original Cooper family farm, abandoned; the bank uses the name out of respect.
- **The Pale Veins** — Hod's ore quarries underground; passages that branch into other things; he stops his stories halfway.

## What This Lore Doesn't Do

- Doesn't explain the bramble (origin, nature, geography).
- Doesn't escalate — the Hedgemother is the largest threat; there is no Worse Bramble.
- Doesn't bind the player — every lore piece is reward for curiosity, never a gate.
- Doesn't flip to grimdark — the imps are exactly what they appear to be.
