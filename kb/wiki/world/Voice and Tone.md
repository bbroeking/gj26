# Voice and Tone

The writing voice for all player-visible text in Wayfinder — dialog, quest copy, item flavor, log lines, location signs — is **warm storybook with short sentences and soft humor**, governed by the [[Bramblewood]] World Bible (locked 2026-04-29).

## The Reference Triangle

> **Hayao Miyazaki + Beatrix Potter + Wind Waker.**

- **Miyazaki:** sincerity earned by contrast; magic that is never explained; characters who carry weight quietly.
- **Beatrix Potter:** plain English, short sentences, animal-kingdom scale of stakes, dry humor with a straight face.
- **Wind Waker:** warmth, wide skies, low-key whimsy that doesn't wink at the audience.

The locked UI aesthetic — cream paper, brown ink, sage and terracotta watercolor washes — is the visual half of this tone. Writing must match the look.

## Core Rules

**Warm, not gritty.** No edge, no irony at the setting's expense. The world is genuinely gentle.

**Whimsical, not satirical.** Humor comes from situation and character, not meta-commentary.

**Low stakes by default.** The biggest crisis is "Maud's pie tin is missing again." *Occasional* moments of sincerity — a village funeral, a worried mother — earn their weight precisely because the baseline is so calm.

**Storybook prose.** Short sentences. Soft humor. Characters repeat themselves the same way every time you talk to them (this is intentional — it is the rhythm of storybooks, not a limitation).

**No grimdark.** No graphic violence, no body horror, no swearing harder than "blast." Bramble-imp bites are described as "a nasty pinch." If it belongs in a gritty ARPG lore-dump, it does not belong here.

## Naming Voice

- **People:** plain English given names + hedge/herb/textile surnames. *Pennycress, Tenter, Withering, Sallow, Cooper.* If it sounds like a generated fantasy name (*Ahnaen, Gwynndor*), discard it.
- **Places:** descriptive compound or possessive. *Pennycress Pasture, Old Mother Well, Sallow's End, Trelliswick Folly.*
- **Skills/verbs:** unfussy English. *Cooking* not *Hearthcraft*. *Smithing* not *Ironworking.* Skills are verbs, not titles.
- **Enemies:** all imp-family names use thorn/berry/hedge words: *thorn-imp, bramble-cap, Hedgemother.*
- **Resources:** original names only, never OSRS names — the locked taxonomy (ore: mosswort, bogiron, coalrose… meat: brindle_roast, pippin_spit…) defines the vocabulary.

## NPC Dialog Samples (from `docs/WORLD_BIBLE.md`)

> *"Three cooked beef. Don't burn 'em. Off you go."* — Maud Pennycress

> *"Hmph. Bring me ore. Bring me coin. Don't waste my fire."* — Old Hod Tenter

> *"Bird's name? Linnet. Don't startle her."* — Sir Withering of Trelliswick

> *"We don't tame the brambles. We tend them. There's a difference."* — Maud Pennycress

> *"Some kindnesses are too big to carry. So we plant them, and let the kindness be the place."* — Mother Onywyn

Pattern: **blunt opening → brief elaboration → dismissal or pivot.** Characters know their own business and assume you do too.

## Lore Flavor Samples (from `docs/WORLD_LORE.md`)

> *"Brother Pell would prefer I sleep at night. I prefer Brother Pell prefer that."* — Mother Onywyn

Folk-tale cadence: matter-of-fact about impossible things, never awed by them.

> "Children claim there's a man asleep down there, breathing. Adults laugh. (There isn't. The village laughs because there is.)"

Codex-entry voice: short paragraph, one wry parenthetical, stops before it explains.

## Quest Copy Pattern

Old (generic): *"Slay 3 cows for the Duke's feast → reward Bronze Sword."*

Bramblewood version: **The Harvest Picnic** — *Maud Pennycress needs three cooked beef for the village lunch tomorrow. The dairy cows are penned in the south pasture; bramble-imps have been agitating them. Bring three cooked beef to her door before sundown. She'll thank you with her late husband's bronze sword. "He won't be needing it. You might."*

Rules: same mechanics, a named character, a domestic reason, a soft sentimental hook, no violence-for-its-own-sake framing.

## What Not to Do

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Multi-page mythology explaining the bramble's origin | A folk legend that stops before it answers |
| Save-the-world stakes | Garden-scale stakes (Maud's pie tin) |
| Pseudo-fantasy filler words (*Ahnaen, the Verisaiye*) | Plain English compound names |
| Grim flips ("the imps are actually demons") | They are exactly what they appear to be |
| Irony or winking at the camera | Straight-faced warmth |
| OSRS metal/herb names | The locked Bramblewood taxonomy |

## Scope of These Rules

All player-visible writing follows the Bible. **This wiki is internal/analytic** — plain, direct language is fine here; no need to write wiki pages in storybook style. The rules apply to: NPC dialog, quest text, item names and flavor text, location signs, codex entries, log lines, any UI string the player reads.
