## Super Metroid (1994, Nintendo R&D1 / Intelligent Systems, SNES)

- Director: Yoshio Sakamoto. Producer: Makoto Kano. Co-development: Intelligent Systems (programming). Staff ~15, supervised by Gunpei Yokoi.
- Custom SNES engine (no public engine).
- Shipped March 1994 (JP), July 1994 (NA/EU).
- Sales: ~1.42 million copies worldwide by late 2003.
- First game in series with: in-game automap, inventory screen, omnidirectional fire.
- Three-act pacing: guided linear tour → open exploration with new tools → boss gauntlet.
- "Invisible hand" design: tutorial rooms mirror future use-locations; no explicit waypoints.
- Ability-gating triggers "upgrade plot twist" — player mentally replays all prior rooms.
- Atmospheric biomes via gameplay difficulty density, not just music (Lower Norfair).
- World always allows detours; every path has a back door (no soft-locks by design).
- Zebes biomes: Crateria, Brinstar, Norfair, Wrecked Ship, Maridia, Tourian.
- Co-credited as genre founder alongside SotN for "Metroidvania."

## Castlevania: Symphony of the Night (1997, Konami Computer Entertainment Tokyo, PS1/Saturn)

- Directed by Toru Hagihara. Koji Igarashi = assistant director, programmer, story. His breakout credit.
- Originated as cancelled Sega 32X title "Castlevania: The Bloodletting" — pivot freed team from series conventions.
- Shipped March 1997 (JP), October 1997 (NA).
- Sales: 700,000+ units US + Japan combined. Launched poorly, became sleeper hit via word of mouth.
- Metacritic: 93/100 PS1.
- Key innovations: EXP leveling + equipment on top of Metroid-style ability traversal; dual gating (ability AND stat level).
- Alucard transformations (wolf/mist/bat) = traversal ability gates. Relics + familiars = passive bonus layer.
- Inverted castle: mirrored second castle with harder enemies, unlocked after beating main castle — content-doubling with ~zero new art.
- Non-linear hub: one contiguous interconnected world, no level select.
- Inspired by Legend of Zelda and Super Metroid per Igarashi.
- RPG stat ramp creates slow-start → power-fantasy arc.

## Ori and the Blind Forest (2015, Moon Studios / Microsoft Studios, PC / Xbox One)

- Director/writer/designer: Thomas Mahler (ex-Blizzard cinematic artist). Co-founder: Gennadiy Korol (ex-senior graphics engineer).
- Distributed virtual studio — team globally remote, multi-disciplinary, ~20 people.
- Engine: Unity (started in Construct, switched early). Target: 1080p / 60fps on Xbox One and PC simultaneously.
- Development: ~4 years total. Published by Microsoft Studios with near-total creative freedom.
- Reception: Best Debut at 2016 GDC Awards. Sequel: Ori and the Will of the Wisps (2020).
- "Animation Design" workflow: produce throwaway concept videos testing movement feel BEFORE committing to production animation. Functions like gameplay storyboarding.
- Explicitly inspired by Super Metroid, Symphony of the Night, Super Meat Boy.
- Composer Gareth Coker embedded from pre-production; fully-scored animatics edited like film.
- Dynamic music reacts to stakes; hand-painted backgrounds use color/light to signal traversability without HUD.
- Escape sequences (Ginso Tree, Forlorn Ruins) = timed platformer gauntlets as narrative setpieces.
- Custom save system: "Soul Link" — player spends Ori's energy to place a checkpoint anywhere.

- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ori_and_the_Blind_Forest

## Celeste (2018, Maddy Makes Games, PC / PS4 / Xbox One / Switch)

- Director/producer: Maddy Thorson. Lead programmer: Noel Berry. Composer: Lena Raine.
- Engine: Microsoft XNA (chosen for frame-level input precision control).
- Origin: PICO-8 prototype built in 4 days (August 2015 game jam). Full game: ~2 years development.
- Shipped January 2018. Sales: 1.7 million copies by early 2025. Metacritic: 92–94/100 across platforms.
- Core moveset: run, jump, wall-climb, 8-directional mid-air dash. No ability gates — depth via room design.
- Key forgiveness mechanics (documented publicly by Thorson):
  - Coyote time: jump valid for a few frames after leaving ledge.
  - Jump buffering: holding jump before landing triggers immediate jump on contact.
  - Corner correction: jump and dash auto-nudge around ledge corners.
  - Halved gravity at jump apex: holding jump reduces gravity at peak.
  - Lift momentum storage: platform velocity transfers to jump, persists briefly.
  - Wall-jump proximity: standard 2px away; super wall-jump 5px+ away.
- Overarching principle: "widening timing/positioning windows so everything is fudged a tiny bit in the player's favor."
- Assist Mode: reduced speed, unlimited dash, invincibility — implemented late in development after Cuphead difficulty discourse.
- Protagonist Madeline confirmed transgender by Thorson in 2020.

## Blasphemous (2019, The Game Kitchen / Team17, PC / PS4 / Xbox One / Switch)

- Creative director: Enrique Cabeza. Level designer: Enrique Colinet. Studio: Seville, Spain.
- Funded via Kickstarter 2017. Published by Team17. Engine: Unity.
- Shipped September 2019. Reached 1 million players by March 2021. Metacritic: 76–82/100.
- Aesthetic: Spanish Catholic religious iconography, Goya paintings, grotesque horror.
- World: Cvstodia. Hierarchy: Districts → Zones → Scenes (screen-based, not scrolling).
  - Switched from scrolling to screen-based design mid-2018 for collaboration and performance reasons.
- Exploration gating: levers, breakable cases, teleport fast-travel, elevators — no hard ability gates.
- Colinet: "we don't want players to backtrack or explore areas if they don't want to."
- Guided non-linearity: linear combat tutorial section, then first real branch at the Wasteland.
- Checkpoint rule: one per area, never more than seven screens apart.
- Soulslike elements in game 1: Tears of Atonement currency lost on death, instant-kill hazards, parry system.
- Blasphemous 2 (2023) removed death-penalty loss and instant-kills; leaned harder into Metroidvania.
- Post-launch patch replaced a gap with a stepladder to guide players toward easier first branch.
