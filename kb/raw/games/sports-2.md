### Key facts
- Released May 1, 2018 (Steam, Oculus Rift). Developer: Beat Games (Czech Republic), acquired by Meta/Facebook 2019.
- Blocks stream toward player on a 4×3 grid; colored directional arrows indicate required swing direction and hand.
- Scoring is entirely motion-based, NOT timing-based:
  - Pre-swing arc ≥100° → up to 70 pts
  - Follow-through arc ≥60° → up to 30 pts
  - Cut centrality → up to 15 pts
  - Max 115 pts/block; 8× combo multiplier for sustained runs
- "Instructed Motion" design principle: directional arrow constraints teach specific physical movements; without constraints, swings would be arbitrary flails.
- Custom maps: BeastSaber (bsaber.com) curates community maps; PC tools include BS Manager, Mod Assistant; Quest requires QuestPatcher/ModsBeforeFriday; Beat Sage is AI auto-mapper from YouTube audio.
- Official game does not support custom songs natively; PC Steam is broadly moddable.
- Practice Mode allows slow-motion isolated-section replay.
- Latency: since scoring ignores timing, VR frame-timing variance is less punishing than in traditional rhythm games.

## Guitar Hero III

### Sources fetched
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_Hero_III:_Legends_of_Rock
- https://www.gamespot.com/articles/guitar-hero-iii-strums-up-1-billion/1100-6203056/
- https://guitarhero.neoseeker.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics
- https://www.punogames.com/blog/guitar-hero-iii-legends-of-rock-review/

### Key facts
- Released October 28, 2007. Developer: Neversoft. Publisher: Activision.
- First game in series since Harmonix moved to Rock Band; Neversoft took over after Activision bought RedOctane.
- Core: guitar-shaped controller, 5 fret buttons, strum bar, whammy bar. Notes scroll down 5-lane highway; hit on crossing the strike line.
- Note types: single notes, sustained notes (hold fret), hammer-ons/pull-offs (no re-strum required), chords (2–4 buttons simultaneously).
- Star Power: earned by playing starred-note sequences; activated by tilting guitar vertically or pressing select button. Effect: doubles scoring multiplier (up to 8×); performance meter more forgiving while active. Strategic timing decision: when to burn the gauge.
- 4 difficulty levels: Easy (3 active lanes), Medium (4 lanes), Hard (5 lanes, includes orange), Expert (5 lanes, full density/speed).
- Performance meter: fills on correct hits, drains on misses; empty = song fail ("booed off stage").
- First online multiplayer in the series (PS3, Wii, Xbox 360).
- Multiplayer modes: Face-Off (individual score), Co-Op Career (lead + rhythm/bass), Battle Mode (Star Power charges attacks vs opponent), Competitive Co-Op.
- Boss fights: Slash, Tom Morello, Lou the Devil — character-based narrative framing around abstract scoring.
- 73 songs; 54 master recordings (vs. earlier games' heavy reliance on covers).
- First retail video game to exceed $1 billion in sales (per Activision). Opening week: $100M+. By July 2008: 8M+ copies.
- Wireless Gibson Les Paul controllers bundled per platform.
- Developer note: hammer-ons/pull-offs placed manually per note by team of 30 musicians.

## Hi-Fi Rush

### Sources fetched
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi-Fi_Rush
- https://gameonreviews.com/feel-the-beat-mastering-rhythm-and-action-in-hi-fi-rush-a-deep-dive/

- https://thenewshouse.com/entertainment/hi-fi-rush-video-game-review/
- WebSearch: "Hi-Fi Rush beat snap accessibility no-fail rhythm optional Tango Gameworks Johanas 2023"

### Key facts
- Released January 25, 2023. Developer: Tango Gameworks. Publisher: Bethesda Softworks.
- Director: John Johanas (previously The Evil Within 2). Development began 2018, kept secret alongside Ghostwire: Tokyo.
- Inspired by Shaun of the Dead (Edgar Wright films) and Futurama.
- Engine: Unreal Engine.
- Setting: Chai's music player is accidentally fused to his chest; the entire world pulses to the music's BPM.
- Beat system: ALL attacks, dodges, parries can be executed at any time; landing them ON the beat amplifies damage, charges combo finisher faster, improves end-level rank.
- Beat-snap: engine INTERPOLATES player inputs to nearest beat frame — inputs never discarded, just snapped to the grid. Very generous hit window by default.
- Combo chains: partner characters can join for call-and-response timed sequences; mastering chains is primary depth layer.
- Environmental design: ALL background assets bounce, bob, and strobe to BPM. Provides a constant visual metronome independent of audio.
- Companion 808 (robotic cat) visibly pulses on-screen as metronome.
- Enemy attack patterns are telegraphed on-beat; reading music = reading combat.
- Aesthetic: cel-shaded, compared to Jet Set Radio. Vivid and arresting.
- Soundtrack: licensed (The Black Keys, Nine Inch Nails) + original compositions by ex-Konami/Capcom composers.
- Accessibility features:
  - Companion Beat Guide: overlay showing live beat waveform.
  - Rhythm Assist: visualize beat on-screen at all times.
  - Auto-Action Mode: optimal attack on any button press.
  - Single Key Rhythm Game Mode: rhythm minigame inputs collapsed to one button.
  - Colorblind filters: Protanopia, Deuteranopia, Tritanopia with adjustable strength.
  - Difficulty changeable mid-game.
- Reception: 90/100 Metacritic (PC), 87 (Xbox Series X/S), 88 (PS5). 3 million players by August 2023. Day-one Xbox Game Pass.
- Criticism: repetitive level design, limited licensed track variety.

## Osu!

### Sources fetched
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osu!
- https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/History_of_osu!/2007
- https://handwiki.org/wiki/Software:Osu!
- https://osugame.fandom.com/wiki/Game_modes
- WebSearch: "osu! game modes standard mania taiko catch history pip performance points ranked beatmap"
- WebSearch: "osu! peppy Dean Herbert created 2007 ranked beatmap history community growth statistics"

### Key facts
- Created by Dean "peppy" Herbert in 2007 as a personal hobby project in New Zealand.
- Public launch: September 17, 2007 via bemanistyle announcement; online rankings went live October 6, 2007.
- Free-to-play; optional supporter subscription funds server costs. Open-source client (osulazer on GitHub).
- 26 million+ registered users as of November 2025.
- 4 game modes:
  1. osu!standard (2007): click circles, hold sliders, spin spinners on-screen to music. Mouse/tablet primary input.
  2. osu!taiko (May 2008): drum-hit simulation on a conveyor belt; based on Taiko no Tatsujin.
  3. osu!catch (February 2010): catcher character catches falling fruit to the beat.
  4. osu!mania (October 2012): vertical-scrolling piano/VSRG mode; inspired by DDR and Beatmania.
- Beatmaps: community-authored charts. Mapper places circles/sliders/spinners in built-in editor aligned to song.
- Beatmap ranking pipeline: mapper submits → Beatmap Nominators (volunteer BNG) peer-review against Ranking Criteria (quality standard covering density, readability, audio sync, difficulty gradient) → 2 nominations → Ranked status.
- Only Ranked beatmaps award Performance Points (pp).
- pp system: computed from map difficulty + accuracy; aggregated across player's top plays into a global rank. Designed to incentivize climbing to harder maps rather than grinding easy maps.
- pp algorithm rebuilt multiple times to close score-exploitation vectors.
- Latency sensitivity: competitive top-tier play benefits from sub-10ms input-to-display. Monitor refresh rate and input lag are meaningfully impactful.
- No VR mode; desktop game (mouse, tablet, keyboard, touch).
- osu! Museum: community-assembled digital archive of maps, players, mapping scenes.
- Ranked/Unranked content split: ranked = pp eligible; unranked = play only, no progression.

## Tetris Effect Connected

### Sources fetched

- WebSearch: "Tetris Effect Connected Zone mechanic design Enhance Games Mizuguchi synesthesia music"
- WebSearch: "Tetris Effect Connected co-op Zone mechanic design multiplayer"

### Key facts
- Developer: Enhance Games. Producer: Tetsuya Mizuguchi. Engine: proprietary Synesthesia Engine (also powers Rez, Lumines, Child of Eden).
- Original Tetris Effect: November 2018, PS4-exclusive. Connected update: October 2020 (Xbox/PC). Full multiplatform: 2021.
- Core design: Synesthesia Engine binds music tempo, intensity, particle animations, haptics, and environment to real-time gameplay state (not scripted triggers). Clears → music intensifies → visual spectacle expands. Slows fall speed when many events fire simultaneously.
- Zone mechanic:
  - Built by the main programmer as a distinct differentiator from other Tetris games.
  - Player builds Zone meter by clearing lines.
  - Activate Zone: Tetriminos freeze mid-fall; time stops.
  - While Zone active: completed lines push to bottom of matrix instead of vanishing; accumulate up to 16 lines.
  - Zone expiration: all accumulated lines clear simultaneously.
  - "Decahexatris" = 16 lines in one Zone activation — ultimate achievement.
  - Dual use: escape hatch when stack is critical, OR amplified scoring window for skill players.
- Relax Mode: removes failure states entirely; optional.
- PSVR support at launch: notably low latency relative to flat-screen; VR version considered premium experience.
- Connected multiplayer modes (Connected update, Oct 2020):
  - Connected (co-op): up to 3 players; individual matrices merge into one wide shared field to fight AI bosses.
    - Boss fills attack meter from its own clears → fires blitz modifiers (negative random effects) at team.
    - Team fills shared Zone meter via their line clears → unleashes Zone attacks on boss.
    - Boss toggles: Attack (sending blitzes) vs Defense (cushion absorbs Zone attacks).
  - Zone Battle (1v1 competitive): classic Tetris multiplayer + Zone mechanic as swing mechanic.
  - Classic Score Attack: traditional Tetris scoring.
- Synesthesia design lineage: Rez (2001) → Lumines (2004) → Child of Eden (2010) → Tetris Effect (2018).
- Critical reception: praised universally; Nintendo Life called it "Mizuguchi's masterpiece" on Switch.

## Cross-cutting notes
- Beat Saber and Hi-Fi Rush both demonstrate that lenient/motion-based scoring (rather than strict timing) dramatically broadens audience while preserving depth for skilled players.
- Guitar Hero III's Star Power and Tetris Effect's Zone are structurally similar: a secondary resource earned via skilled play, held strategically, then activated for a burst of amplified feedback and reward.
- Osu!'s ranked/unranked content split and pp aggregation system are the clearest model for "community content with quality tiers" — applicable to Wayfinder's chart ecosystem.
- Hi-Fi Rush's accessibility suite (beat-snap interpolation, single-button modes, mid-game difficulty change) is the most transferable onboarding lesson across all five games.
