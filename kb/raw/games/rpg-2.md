## The Elder Scrolls III Morrowind

**Sources fetched:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrowind
- https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Skills_(Morrowind)
- https://www.thegamer.com/morrowind-skills-attributes-explained/
- https://www.thecourieronline.co.uk/mechanical-focus-rpgs-and-the-mastery-of-morrowind/
- https://retromo.substack.com/p/morrowind-pc-2002

**Key facts extracted:**
- Released 2002, Bethesda Game Studios, NetImmerse engine, PC + Xbox.
- 27 skills in three specializations (Combat, Magic, Stealth); major/minor/misc tripartite structure.
- Use-based leveling: practicing a skill increases it; 10 points in major/minor skills = level up, with attribute multipliers tied to which skills were practiced (1x–5x range).
- Custom class creation lets players mix-and-match regardless of specialization.
- Unleveled open world: enemy/loot difficulty fixed to region, not player level. Creates organic exploration difficulty gradient.
- Core design philosophy (Ken Rolston): "free-form experience." NPCs use keyword dialogue, auto-journaling hundreds of entries.
- 89/100 Metacritic PC; 4 million+ copies by 2005.
- OpenMW: open-source engine reimplementation still active in 2026.
- Legacy: foundational for modding communities; Skyrim simplified the system substantially.
- Morrowind's unleveled world means early areas can be nearly impossible for weak characters; late areas trivially easy — acknowledged weakness.

## Dragon Age Origins

**Sources fetched:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Age:_Origins
- https://dragonage.fandom.com/wiki/Companions_(Origins)
- https://dragonage.fandom.com/wiki/Approval_(Origins) (HTTP 403 — used search results)
- https://www.thegamer.com/dragon-age-origins-companion-approval-guide/
- https://dragonage.miraheze.org/wiki/Approval_(Origins)

**Key facts extracted:**
- Released 2009, BioWare / EA, Eclipse Engine, PC/Xbox 360/PS3/Mac.
- Six origin stories: human noble, dwarf commoner/noble, city elf, Dalish elf, mage; each gives unique opening chapter and leaves trace reactions throughout campaign.
- Eight companions with approval scores (−100 to +100); thresholds unlock: dialogue, personal quests, attribute bonuses (Wynne: Willpower; Sten: Strength at high approval), romance.
- Cross-companion conflict: Alistair and Morrigan have opposing values; pleasing both requires negotiation.
- Tactics menu: per-companion scripted condition/action AI (up to ~12 slots depending on level); pausable real-time combat.
- Three classes → three specializations each; stamina/mana resource; sustained abilities toggle.
- 68,260 lines of dialogue; 144 voice actors; Inon Zur score with 44-piece orchestra.
- 91/100 Metacritic PC; 3.2M+ copies; three sequels.
- Personal quests gate behind "Warm" approval threshold; romance requires companion quest completion.
- Approval system has no rivalry track (added in DA2); pure positive/negative score.

## Deus Ex

**Sources fetched:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deus_Ex_(video_game)
- https://www.inverse.com/gaming/deus-ex-anniversary-immersive-sim-warren-spector-system-shock
- https://pcgamezusa.com/blogs/reviews/deus-ex-2000-a-timeless-classic-that-redefined-rpgs
- https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusEx

**Key facts extracted:**
- Released June 2000, Ion Storm (Austin) / Eidos, Unreal Engine 1.
- Warren Spector: described as "ultimate realization" of RPG-shooter-stealth synthesis; built on System Shock, Thief, Ultima Underworld heritage.
- Two progression layers: 11 Skills (Computers, Lockpicking, Demolitions, Swimming, Medicine, weapons) improved by Skill Points from quests; Augmentation canisters slotted into body zones (head, eyes, torso, arms, legs, subdermal) unlocking new verbs.
- Level design: every major area offers stealth, hacking, combat, bribery, vent/alternate-route solutions using same underlying systems.
- Faction play: UNATCO → NSF → Majestic 12 → Illuminati; three distinct endings.
- Immersive sim principle: no scripted fixed events; consistent rules throughout (if you can knock a civilian out, you can knock a general out).
- 90/100 Metacritic; consistently ranked among best games ever made.
- AI coding was late-stage development crisis; female player character and White House/space station levels cut.
- Three sequels: Invisible War (2003), Human Revolution (2011), Mankind Divided (2016).

## Planescape Torment

**Sources fetched:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planescape:_Torment
- https://medium.com/@adamjohnrobertson/what-can-change-the-nature-of-a-man-c1d9e3d08556
- https://anastst.medium.com/i-played-planescape-torment-in-2025-to-discover-it-can-indeed-change-the-nature-of-a-man-e19cd7e882f8
- https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-97134-6_94-2
- https://thesecretlevel.net (certificate expired — not fetched)

**Key facts extracted:**
- Released December 1999, Black Isle Studios / Interplay, modified Infinity Engine (Baldur's Gate platform).
- ~800,000 words of dialogue; most encounters resolvable through conversation.
- The Nameless One: immortal amnesiac; class switch via NPC conversations (fighter/mage/thief); alignment (good/evil, law/chaos) shifts by decision.
- Stat-gated dialogue: INT, WIS, CHA open distinct solution branches. High CHA can shame NPCs; high INT reveals hidden logic; physical stats govern combat only.
- 15 Planescape factions representing competing metaphysical belief systems; joining gates dialogue and services.
- Six companions, each embodying a thematic "torment" mirroring the protagonist: Morte (skull, denial of death), Dak'kon (enslaved by a vow to past-Nameless-One), Fall-from-Grace (succubus who renounced sensation), Ignus (living fire), Vhailor (embodiment of justice/regret), Annah (tiefling thief).
- 91/100 Metacritic; commercial underperformance at launch; Enhanced Edition 2017 by Beamdog.
- Lead writer Chris Avellone; deliberate argument against combat-first RPG assumption.
- Central question: "What can change the nature of a man?" Canonical answer: regret (giving up immortality to amend harm done).
- Influenced: Disco Elysium, Tyranny, every stat-gated narrative RPG.

## Pillars of Eternity

**Sources fetched:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Eternity
- https://pillarsofeternity.fandom.com/wiki/Skills (HTTP 403 — used search results)
- https://pillarsofeternity.fandom.com/wiki/Attributes

- https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps4/211832-pillars-of-eternity-complete-edition/faqs/79897/character-creation

**Key facts extracted:**
- Released 2015, Obsidian Entertainment / Paradox Interactive, Unity engine.
- Kickstarter: ~$4M from 75,000 backers (2012); first major cRPG Kickstarter success.
- Five skills (Stealth, Athletics, Lore, Mechanics, Survival) operate entirely outside combat — content keys: Mechanics = locks/traps, Lore = scrolls/dialogue wisdom, Athletics = scripted event endurance, Stealth = scouting, Survival = food/potion duration.
- Only Watcher's skills count in dialogue checks (companions' skills do not); forces personal investment in conversation paths.
- Dual resource: Endurance (fast, per-fight recovery) + Health (slow drain, per-rest recovery). Running out of Health = incapacitated until rest. Campaign-level resource management.
- 11 classes; universal attributes (Might governs damage AND healing; Resolve = concentration + deflection for all classes) — no dump stats.
- Reputation/disposition system: tracks Honest, Clever, Benevolent, Aggressive, Cruel across factions; epilogues reflect world state.
- Crafting: ingredients + character level only; no separate crafting skill.
- 89/100 Metacritic PC; 700K+ units by Feb 2016. Sequel: Deadfire (2018); spiritual successor Avowed (2025, first-person).
- Party specialization model: one character should own Mechanics, one Stealth, Watcher maxes Lore; discourages redundancy, encourages role differentiation.
