## Guacamelee! 2 (2018, DrinkBox Studios, PS4 / PC / Switch / Xbox One)

- Developed and published by DrinkBox Studios (Toronto). PS4 + PC: August 21 2018; Switch: December 10 2018; Xbox One: January 18 2019.
- Engine: proprietary DrinkBox C++ engine (upgraded from original). Original game: team of ~5–13 over 8 months preproduction + 11 months production.
- Metacritic: 83–87/100 across platforms. No publicly confirmed sales figures.
- Core concept: Mexican lucha libre × Metroidvania. Cultural lead Augusto Quijano (born in Mexico) designed the aesthetic.
- All combat moves double as traversal abilities: dash, uppercut, slam, throw work for both fighting and platforming. This unification was validated with a Flash prototype before production.
- Dimension-shift (Darkest Timeline): toggles between living and shadow world; some enemies/platforms only exist in one dimension.
- Chicken form expanded in sequel: gains Juan's full moveset (throw, wrestling moves, wall-jump, dodge) plus chicken-exclusive moves (slow fall, diagonal dash, ground slide). The "Pollovania" is a nested metroidvania inside the main game.
- 4-player local co-op added (original had 2). Extra players play as alternate luchadors.
- Ability colors code both combat and traversal: colored wall targets correspond to matching punch type; gates player into new areas once ability unlocked.
- Design iteration philosophy: "We began by looking at all of the places we thought the original game could be improved or extended." — Graham Smith (DrinkBox).

## Metroid Dread (2021, MercurySteam + Nintendo EPD, Nintendo Switch)

- Co-developed by MercurySteam (Spain) and Nintendo EPD. Director: Yoshio Sakamoto (original Metroid series director). Published Nintendo. Released October 8 2021.
- Originally conceived for Nintendo DS (~2005); cancelled due to hardware limitations. Revived after MercurySteam's Metroid: Samus Returns (3DS, 2017) proved the partnership.
- First original side-scrolling Metroid game since Metroid Fusion (2002). First 2D Metroid in HD.
- Metacritic: 88/100. Won Best Action/Adventure at The Game Awards 2021.
- Sales: 3 million+ copies (as of December 2022). Fastest-selling Metroid in Japan, UK, and US.
- E.M.M.I. (Extraplanetary Multiform Mobile Identifier): zone-specific robots that patrol sealed corridors; immune to normal weapons, kill on catch. Player uses stealth (Phantom Cloak), environment, timing. Defeated via context-specific Omega Cannon (temporary ability per zone).
- Sakamoto: each EMMI color corresponds to a classic Metroid ability the robot wields. Humanoid-on-all-fours design taps primal fear; locomotion cues from Exorcist-style contortion; detection sound modeled on oriental turtle dove cry.
- EMMI shift arc: player enters zone hunted → learns layout → defeats EMMI → absorbs its ability and exits empowered. Mirrors traditional Metroid power arc compressed into one zone.
- Traditional ability gating retained outside EMMI zones. Phantom Cloak, Spider Magnet, Flash Shift, Spin Boost all gate areas.
- MercurySteam's HD experience was pivotal; Sakamoto's team "had a hard time figuring out what quality to shoot for" in HD 2D.
- Omega Cannon shifted perspective to third-person for dramatic framing — an unusual choice in 2D Metroid.

## Axiom Verge (2015, Thomas Happ Games, PS4 / PC / Wii U / Switch / Xbox One / Vita)

- Sole developer: Thomas Happ (developer, artist, composer). Began March 2010 as side project while employed at Petroglyph Games. ~5 years development.
- Engine: Microsoft XNA (Windows); MonoGame (other platforms). PS4 launch: March 31 2015 (NA); PC: May 14 2015.
- Metacritic: 84/100 (PS4), 89/100 (Wii U). Giant Bomb: 5/5.
- Aesthetic and design draw explicitly from NES Metroid and Contra. 60+ weapons/power-ups.
- Signature mechanic: Address Disruptor ("glitch gun") fires waves of blue rectangles that corrupt enemies (making them easier or behaviorally different) and blocks (making them passable or impassable). Named for memory-address hacking.
- Passcode Tool: inspired by NES Metroid's password system; players find in-world strings that unlock secrets and sequence breaks. Encourages experimentation and community sharing.
- Glitch World: hidden layer of the map accessible via specific glitch moves; contains meta-commentary and lore fragments.
- Drone: deployable unit that can access areas too small for Trace, navigate alternate passages, trigger remote switches.
- Happ sketched tileset layouts on paper for months before writing code — art-first dev on a solo project.
- Sequel Axiom Verge 2 (August 2021) also solo-developed by Happ.

## Shovel Knight (2014, Yacht Club Games, PC / 3DS / Wii U / PS4 / Xbox One / Switch)

- Developed and published by Yacht Club Games (Los Angeles). Director: Sean Velasco. Small team (~12–15 core). Kickstarter: raised $311,502 (target $75,000); May 2013 campaign.
- Engine: 3D engine used for easier porting and bug fixing behind a 2D camera (mimics NES appearance but not hardware).
- PC / 3DS / Wii U: June 26 2014; PS4: April 2015. Sales: 2.65 million copies by September 2019.
- Metacritic: 85–92/100 across platforms.
- Design research: team studied Mega Man, Castlevania III, DuckTales, Super Mario Bros. 3, Dark Souls to distill their principles.
- NES constraints CHOSEN: ~25–26 screens per level (Mega Man formula), two-button feel, pixel art at NES color depth.
- NES constraints BROKEN: multiple background layers with parallax, elaborate decorative details impossible on real NES hardware.
- Downward thrust (pogo bounce): bouncing off enemies/objects to cross gaps. Primary traversal + combat verb — combines DuckTales pogo with Scrooge McDuck energy.
- Checkpoint flags can be destroyed for gold, but destroying them removes mid-level respawn. Dark Souls tension: risk vs. reward with a binary consequence.
- Gold scatter on death: drop gold coins on death, recoverable at death spot. No lives system — removes frustration, keeps economy stakes.
- Relics: eight secondary abilities (one per boss) powered by magic meter, gating certain secrets.
- Teaching philosophy: "You present new gameplay ideas in a safe situation... You teach A, then B, then put A and B together." — Yacht Club.
- Expanded into Shovel Knight: Treasure Trove anthology — four playable campaigns (Plague of Shadows, Specter of Torment, King of Cards) with distinct mechanics per knight.

## Nine Sols (2024, Red Candle Games, PC / PS4-5 / Switch / Xbox)

- Developer: Red Candle Games (Taiwan; founded ~2015). Third title after Detention (2017) and Devotion (2019). Team grew from 6 founders to 10+ full-time. Development: ~5 years (announced March 22 2022; PC launch May 29 2024; console November 26 2024).
- Engine: Unity. Hand-drawn 2D animation.
- Metacritic: 84/100. Reviewed as one of the tightest 2D action games since Hollow Knight.
- Aesthetic: "Taopunk" — Taoism + cyberpunk. Setting: New Kunlun. Protagonist Yi is inspired by the Chinese archer Hou Yi reimagined in sci-fi context.
- Core combat: deflect-first, not dodge-first. Parrying absorbs Qi; Qi loads explosive talismans placed on enemies; triggered for burst damage. Close-range sword + ranged bow. Wall-running, grapple traversal.
- Nine Sols (bosses): nine named antagonist rulers. Boss fights require learning deflect timing specific to each Sol.
- Narrative-mechanics integration: Yi's arc (vengeance, technology vs. nature, hidden truths) expressed through the escalating difficulty and precision demand of deflection combat.
- Inspirations: Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice (deflect system), Hollow Knight (exploration feel, hand-drawn tone), Katana Zero (narrative style).
- Theme song by Taiwanese indie band Collage, with Taiwanese Hokkien vocals — cultural authenticity as design value.
